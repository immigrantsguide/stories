A tale of honesty, greed and human nature

The Honest Woodcutter

Moral Stories · 8 min read

A peaceful village. A mysterious river. One honest man—and a secret that reveals what people truly value.

The River That Tested Every Human Heart

Long ago, hidden between green mountains and beautiful forests, there was a peaceful village called Sundarpur.

It was one of the most beautiful villages anyone could imagine. Colorful flowers decorated the small pathways, birds sang from the branches of enormous trees, and golden sunlight danced across the rooftops of little houses.

A crystal-clear river flowed beside the village. Its water was so clean that people could see tiny fish swimming between the colorful stones at the bottom.

The river was the heart of Sundarpur. Farmers used its water for their fields. Children played along its banks. Women collected water for their homes, and fishermen returned every evening with baskets full of fish.

Nobody was rich, but everyone had enough to live happily. The villagers believed the river was a blessing from nature.

But nobody knew that the river was hiding a mysterious secret.

The Man Who Lost His Axe

Among the villagers lived a poor woodcutter named Ramu.

He was not a wealthy man. His small house had a broken roof, his clothes were old, and he owned only one iron axe. Every morning, Ramu walked into the forest, collected firewood, and sold it in the village market. Whatever money he earned was used to feed his family.

One afternoon, while cutting a thick branch near the riverbank, something unexpected happened.

His axe slipped from his sweaty hands.

Splash!

It fell straight into the deepest part of the river.

“Oh, no! My axe!” Ramu shouted.

He rushed toward the water and searched desperately, but the river was too deep. He sat beneath a tree and covered his face with his hands.

“How will I work tomorrow? I don't even have enough money to buy another axe.”

Suddenly, the water began to glow.

A mysterious figure emerged from the river. It looked like an old man dressed in shining white clothes, with eyes that seemed to carry the wisdom of centuries.

“Why are you crying, woodcutter?” the mysterious man asked.

Ramu explained what had happened.

The stranger smiled. “Do not worry. I will help you.”

He disappeared beneath the water and returned holding a beautiful golden axe.

“Is this your axe?”

Ramu looked at it in surprise. It was magnificent. Its handle was decorated with precious stones, and its blade shone brighter than the afternoon sun.

But Ramu shook his head. “No, sir. That is not mine.”

The stranger disappeared again. This time, he returned with a silver axe.

“Perhaps this belongs to you?”

Ramu carefully examined it. “No, sir. That is not mine either.”

Finally, the stranger brought out an old iron axe.

Ramu's eyes filled with happiness. “Yes! That is my axe! That is the one I lost!”

The mysterious man smiled. “You could have taken the golden or silver axe. Why did you refuse them?”

Ramu replied, “Because they do not belong to me. My poverty may make my life difficult, but it should never make me dishonest.”

The stranger was deeply pleased. “For your honesty, I will give you all three axes.”

Ramu could hardly believe his ears. He thanked the stranger and returned home with the three axes.

A Story Becomes a Village Sensation

The next morning, Ramu told his neighbors everything that had happened.

At first, nobody believed him. But when they saw the golden and silver axes, their eyes widened.

Within hours, the entire village knew about the mysterious river. People gathered around Ramu's house.

“Are you telling the truth?”

“Did the river really give you gold?”

“Can the same thing happen to us?”

Ramu explained everything. “The river does not belong to us. I don't know why the mysterious man appeared. Please don't disturb the water. I was lucky, and I don't want anyone to misuse this blessing.”

But his warning made people even more curious.

A merchant thought about the gold he could collect. A farmer imagined buying more land. A fisherman wondered whether the river could give him a golden fishing net. Even the village headman began thinking about how much wealth the river might contain.

Soon, curiosity turned into temptation.

The First Test

A young merchant named Gopal decided to test the river.

He took an old, broken iron axe and deliberately threw it into the water. Then he began shouting, “Help! My axe has fallen into the river!”

The mysterious figure appeared.

“Is this your axe?” He showed Gopal a golden axe.

Gopal immediately recognized it as his opportunity. “Yes! Yes! That is mine!” he shouted.

The mysterious man's expression changed. “Are you certain?”

“Of course! It belongs to me!”

The stranger looked directly into his eyes. “You came here with an iron axe. Why are you claiming gold?”

Gopal's smile disappeared. The golden axe turned into dust. The river suddenly became dark. A powerful wave pushed Gopal backward, and his original broken axe was thrown onto the riverbank.

The mysterious man spoke in a cold voice. “You have lost nothing that was yours. But you have lost the opportunity to receive anything more.”

Gopal returned home embarrassed and empty-handed.

The villagers who witnessed the incident became frightened. Some believed the river was magical. Others believed it was testing their honesty.

But not everyone learned the lesson.

When Necessity Becomes an Excuse

A few days later, an old farmer named Mohan approached the river.

His crops had failed, his debts were increasing, and his children had not eaten properly for two days. He had heard what happened to Gopal.

But Mohan thought differently.

“I am not greedy like that merchant,” he told himself. “My family is starving. I only need enough gold to save them.”

He threw his iron axe into the river.

The mysterious man appeared with a golden axe. “Is this yours?”

Mohan stared at it. For a moment, he remembered his children. He remembered their hungry faces and his wife's tears.

Then he slowly nodded. “Yes. It is mine.”

The stranger remained silent. The golden axe disappeared. A silver axe appeared.

“Is this yours?”

Mohan hesitated, then nodded again. The silver axe disappeared too.

Finally, the mysterious man brought out Mohan's original iron axe.

Mohan lowered his head. “Yes. That is mine.”

The stranger handed it to him. Then he asked a question that Mohan would never forget.

“Tell me, farmer. If your family was hungry, would you steal food from another hungry family and call it necessity?”

Mohan had no answer.

The stranger continued. “Need can explain your struggle, but it cannot turn a lie into the truth.”

Mohan began crying. “I am sorry. I was afraid for my family.”

The stranger looked at him kindly. “Then return home. Your family deserves food, but not through dishonesty.”

He disappeared into the river.

That evening, the villagers helped Mohan by sharing food and grain. For the first time, Mohan understood that asking for help was not shameful. Pretending something belonged to him was.

The Secret Nobody Expected

As days passed, more villagers visited the river.

Some threw away their tools. Others dropped old jewelry, broken pots, and even farming equipment into the water. Everyone wanted to discover the river's powers.

The mysterious figure appeared to some, ignored others, and occasionally returned only what they had lost.

But something strange began happening.

The river was becoming shallower. The fish were disappearing. The water that once flowed like a silver ribbon had started turning muddy.

Ramu noticed it first. He rushed to the village meeting.

“Something is wrong! We must stop throwing things into the river. It is losing its water!”

But the villagers laughed.

“Don't worry, Ramu. The river has always been here.”

“Perhaps there is more gold beneath the surface!”

“We should explore before someone else discovers it!”

The village headman even organized a group to search for the mysterious river treasure. They brought ropes, baskets, digging tools, and large containers.

They searched every corner of the river. Some wanted gold. Some wanted silver. Others simply wanted to see what would happen.

Nobody wanted to listen.

The River's Final Warning

One evening, the sky turned unusually dark. The birds stopped singing. The wind disappeared.

The villagers gathered around the river for another search.

Suddenly, the water began moving in circles.

A deep voice echoed across the riverbank.

“STOP!”

Everyone froze.

The mysterious figure appeared one final time. His face looked sad.

“For generations, this river has given your village life. It gave you water, food, happiness, and peace. Yet the moment you discovered that it could give you something more, you stopped respecting what you already had.”

The village headman stepped forward. “Who are you? Why did you give Ramu three axes but refuse to give us gold?”

The mysterious man answered, “Ramu needed his axe to earn his living. He did not ask for wealth. He did not demand gold. He simply told the truth.”

He looked at the crowd. “But you came here not to appreciate the river. You came to test how much you could take from it.”

A young villager shouted, “Then give us one chance! We promise we will change!”

The stranger looked at him. “How many chances does a human heart need before it understands the value of what it already possesses?”

Nobody spoke.

Then the stranger raised his hand. The river became completely still. A bright light emerged from beneath the water. For a moment, everyone could see hundreds of golden objects lying at the bottom.

The villagers rushed forward.

But before anyone could reach them, the light vanished.

The water began sinking. Slowly at first. Then faster.

Within minutes, the river that had supported Sundarpur for generations became a narrow stream.

The mysterious man disappeared.

And the river never returned to its former glory.

The Last Question

Years passed. The beautiful village changed.

The green fields became dry. The fishermen left. Children no longer played along the riverbank.

The villagers had once been happy with clean water, green trees, and enough food. Now they had neither gold nor the river that had given them everything.

One afternoon, an old Ramu sat beside the nearly dry riverbed.

A little boy approached him. “Grandfather, why did the river disappear?”

Ramu looked at the empty riverbank. He picked up a small stone and placed it in the dry bed.

“The river did not disappear because it had no water, child. It disappeared because we forgot how to value it.”

The boy looked confused. “But why did everyone want gold when they already had everything?”

Ramu smiled sadly.

“Because humans often change their desires according to what they see, what they need, and what they believe they are missing.”

The boy asked one final question. “Then will humans ever change?”

Ramu looked toward the mountains.

“I hope so, my child. But remember one thing. When people have nothing, they pray for what they need. When they have enough, they begin wanting what others possess. And when they finally get what they want, they sometimes destroy the very thing that made them happy.”

The boy remained silent.

A gentle breeze passed through the empty riverbed. Somewhere far away, a bird sang.

And the village remembered the river that had once given them everything.

The Moral of the Story

Human beings often change their values according to their requirements, necessities, and desires.

When we have nothing, we ask for enough to survive. When we have enough, we start wanting more. When we see someone else receive something valuable, we begin questioning what we already have.

And sometimes, in our curiosity, greed, and endless desire to gain more, we destroy the very blessings that once made our lives beautiful.

The woodcutter was honest because he needed only what belonged to him. The merchant wanted gold because he saw an opportunity. The farmer lied because he believed his necessity justified it. The villagers destroyed the river because they wanted to discover everything it could offer.

Humans never truly change. Their choices change with their needs, their circumstances, and their desires.

Sometimes, the greatest treasure in life is not what we can gain, but what we already have and fail to appreciate.
