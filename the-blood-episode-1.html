THE BLOOD

Three kingdoms. One forbidden bloodline. A child they tried to erase has returned—and the war they feared is only beginning.

SEASON 1EPISODE 1DARK FANTASY

The Beginning of War

The Blood · Season 1, Episode 1 · English

Meet the Characters

Character portraits from The Blood universe.

The night sky turned red.

A blood moon appeared above Rudhiravan, the forbidden village where three supernatural kingdoms shared one land.

That night, the vampire king, the werewolf lord, and the witch queen had gathered inside an ancient temple. They had not come to fight each other. For the first time in a thousand years, they had come together for one purpose.

To kill a child.

A child born from two bloodlines that should never have mixed.

A child whose father belonged to the werewolves and whose mother belonged to the vampires.

His name was Vijay.

The witches had predicted that when he reached adulthood, his power would become greater than all three kingdoms combined.

The vampires feared he would take their throne.

The werewolves feared he would rule the forest.

The witches feared he would destroy their magic.

So they made a secret agreement.

If Vijay could not be controlled, he had to die.

Before midnight, soldiers surrounded his home. The vampires blocked the roads. The werewolves guarded the forest. The witches covered the sky with dark magic.

But something went wrong.

By sunrise, the soldiers had disappeared.

The house had burned to ashes.

Vijay's mother was missing.

His father was declared dead.

And the child was never found.

The three kingdoms celebrated their victory.

They built a monument to remember the night they had defeated the greatest threat to their existence.

But they made one mistake.

They never searched beneath the ashes.

Because beneath those ashes, something was still breathing.

And it remembered every face.

Twenty years later.

The blood moon returned.

Inside the black palace of the vampire king, hundreds of vampires gathered for the annual Blood Festival. Red torches burned along the walls. Ancient warriors stood beside the throne, and the royal family celebrated another year of absolute power.

King Rudraveer sat upon his black throne.

He had ruled for six hundred years. He had defeated rival vampire clans, imprisoned witches, and forced werewolf tribes to retreat into the mountains.

He believed nothing in the world could frighten him.

Then the first bell rang.

GONG!

The entire palace shook.

A second bell rang.

GONG!

Every vampire turned toward the northern gate.

The third bell never rang.

Instead, the guard responsible for sounding it came running into the hall.

His armor was broken. His face was pale. His hands were shaking so badly that he could barely stand.

He fell to his knees.

“Your Majesty!”

Rudraveer looked annoyed.

“Why have you interrupted my festival?”

The guard tried to speak, but no words came out.

The king rose.

“Speak before I remove your tongue.”

The guard finally whispered:

“Someone has crossed the northern boundary.”

The king laughed.

“Send the guards.”

“We did, Your Majesty.”

“And?”

The guard lowered his head.

“None returned.”

The laughter stopped.

Rudraveer walked down the steps of his throne.

“How many soldiers?”

The guard swallowed.

“Three hundred.”

A vampire general stepped forward.

“Impossible. The northern boundary is protected by blood magic. No creature can cross it without the king's permission.”

The guard looked up, tears in his eyes.

“He didn't cross it, General.”

“Then how did he enter?”

The guard's answer made every vampire in the hall go silent.

“The boundary opened for him.”

The king's face changed.

For six hundred years, the boundary had answered only to royal blood.

Someone had just entered his kingdom without breaking a single spell.

Someone whose blood the palace itself recognized.

Rudraveer slowly turned toward the northern windows.

“Who is it?”

The guard looked at the floor.

Then he whispered one name.

“Vijay.”

A wine glass slipped from someone's hand and shattered.

An elderly vampire began repeating an ancient prayer.

The royal general took three steps backward.

And the king, who had never bowed before another creature, quietly sat down.

Because he remembered the massacre.

He remembered the child's burning house.

And he remembered the promise he had made twenty years ago.

If that boy ever returns, I will kill him myself.

The palace doors remained closed.

Outside, the royal guards waited with their weapons raised.

No footsteps could be heard.

No movement could be seen.

Then a voice came from the darkness.

“Open the gates.”

The captain of the royal guard laughed nervously.

“Whoever you are, kneel before the king!”

Silence.

The captain raised his sword.

“I will count to three.”

A low voice answered.

“Count carefully.”

The captain frowned.

“One.”

A torch went out.

“Two.”

Another torch went out.

The guards began looking around.

“Three!”

Every torch along the northern wall went dark at once.

The gate remained closed.

Then something struck it from the other side.

BOOM!

The metal bent inward.

The guards stepped back.

BOOM!

Cracks spread across the ancient gate.

The captain shouted, “Hold your positions!”

A third impact never came.

Instead, a small black object rolled beneath the gate.

It stopped at the captain's feet.

He looked down.

It was the royal seal of the northern commander.

The captain picked it up.

The seal was still warm.

A message had been carved into its surface.

THE KING IS NEXT.

Before he could react, a shadow appeared behind the gate.

The captain raised his sword.

“Attack!”

Hundreds of weapons struck at once.

The gate exploded inward.

But there was nobody behind it.

The soldiers stared into the empty entrance.

Then a voice came from inside the palace.

“Wrong direction.”

Every guard turned.

A young man stood at the top of the staircase leading to the royal hall.

He had already entered the palace.

Nobody had seen him pass.

He wore a long black coat. His hair fell across his forehead, and his face carried a calm, almost bored expression.

His eyes were blood-red.

But deep inside them, a golden light moved like fire.

He looked at the soldiers.

Then he smiled.

“Is this the strongest army in the vampire kingdom?”

Nobody answered.

Vijay sighed.

“Twenty years ago, you were better at killing children.”

The words struck harder than any weapon.

The oldest vampires recognized his face.

Not because he looked exactly as he had twenty years ago.

But because his eyes were the same eyes they had seen through the flames.

The eyes of a child who had watched his entire world burn.

Vijay spread his arms.

“Come on.”

He tilted his head.

“Show me how brave you were that night.”

The royal captain charged first.

He was one of the oldest vampire warriors alive. His sword had killed witches, werewolves, and hundreds of vampires who had challenged the king.

He moved faster than the human eye could follow.

His blade reached Vijay's neck.

A metallic sound echoed through the hall.

The sword had stopped.

Vijay had caught it between two fingers.

The captain stared at him.

Vijay looked at the blade.

“Is this supposed to frighten me?”

He snapped the sword in half.

Before the captain could move, Vijay appeared behind him.

One quiet movement.

The captain fell.

The remaining guards attacked together.

Vijay did not step backward.

He walked forward.

Weapons struck him. Dark magic surrounded him. Silver chains wrapped around his arms.

For one moment, the entire army believed they had caught him.

Then Vijay looked down at the chains.

He smiled.

The metal began to melt.

Not from heat.

From the power inside his blood.

Vijay pulled his arms apart. The chains shattered, sending pieces flying across the hall.

He lifted one hand.

Every vampire in the front line was thrown backward as if an invisible giant had struck them.

The royal mages began chanting.

Vijay listened.

Then he repeated their spell in the same language.

The magic reversed.

Their protective barriers collapsed, and their enchanted weapons turned into dust.

One of the mages screamed, “He has learned the spell!”

The others backed away.

“No,” the oldest mage whispered. “He didn't learn it.”

He stared at Vijay in disbelief.

“He understood it.”

That was worse.

A vampire could be stronger than another vampire.

A werewolf could overpower a vampire.

A witch could use magic against both.

But Vijay was something different.

He could fight like a vampire, strike like a werewolf, and break magic as if it were a child's toy.

And his power was still growing.

Vijay walked toward the throne.

Every step echoed through the hall.

The surviving soldiers moved aside without being ordered.

He looked at them with disappointment.

“Remember this feeling.”

One guard trembled.

“What feeling?”

Vijay leaned closer.

“The moment you realize your king cannot save you.”

Then he continued toward Rudraveer.

Nobody followed him.

Nobody dared.

King Rudraveer rose from his throne.

His fear turned into anger.

“This is my kingdom!”

Dark energy spread across his body. His eyes turned completely black, and enormous shadows rose behind him.

The vampires fell to their knees.

Their king had awakened his ancient form.

Even the witches feared this power.

Rudraveer disappeared.

He reappeared behind Vijay and struck him with enough force to crack the palace floor.

The entire hall shook.

The king attacked again and again, moving faster than the guards could see.

Finally, he drove Vijay into a stone pillar.

The pillar collapsed.

Dust filled the hall.

Rudraveer stood over the wreckage.

“Twenty years ago, I should have burned your body myself.”

A voice answered from behind him.

“You should have checked whether I was breathing.”

Rudraveer turned.

Vijay stood beside the throne, brushing dust from his coat.

Not a single wound remained.

The king stared at him.

Vijay walked closer.

“You have grown old, Uncle.”

“Do not call me that!”

“Why not? You helped raise me.”

“That was before you became a monster!”

Vijay stopped.

For a moment, the smile disappeared from his face.

Then he laughed.

“Before?”

His voice became quiet.

“Tell me, King Rudraveer. At what age does a child become a monster? When he kills his first enemy? When he loses his family? Or when he learns that the people who promised to protect him were the ones who ordered his death?”

The king raised his hand to strike again.

Vijay caught his wrist without even looking at him.

The palace shook.

Every window shattered.

The king tried to pull away.

He could not.

Vijay tightened his grip.

For the first time in six hundred years, the Vampire King was physically powerless before another creature.

Vijay leaned toward his ear.

“I could destroy your kingdom tonight.”

He looked at the terrified vampires surrounding them.

“But that would be too easy.”

He released the king.

Rudraveer dropped to one knee, struggling to recover his strength.

Vijay turned toward the throne and placed one hand on its black surface.

“Here is your punishment.”

He looked at the king.

“You will keep your crown.”

The king slowly raised his head.

“You will keep your army. You will keep your palace. And you will tell your people that you are still their greatest ruler.”

Vijay smiled.

“Because every night you sleep in this palace, you will wonder when I am coming back.”

He leaned forward.

“And one day, when you finally believe you are safe…”

His eyes flashed gold.

“I will stand behind you again.”

He turned to leave.

Then he stopped at the doorway.

“One more thing.”

The entire hall froze.

Vijay looked over his shoulder.

“Find my mother.”

The king's expression changed.

It was only for a second.

But Vijay noticed.

His smile vanished.

“Oh,” he whispered.

“So you know where she is.”

Rudraveer said nothing.

Vijay nodded slowly.

“Now this becomes interesting.”

He walked out of the palace.

The guards moved aside.

No one attempted to stop him.

And as he crossed the northern boundary, the ancient gates opened for him once more.

The vampires had not lost a battle.

They had received a warning.

At that exact moment, three impossible things happened.

In the witches' temple, every candle went out.

Queen Mahakaali dropped the ancient mirror she had held for four hundred years. It shattered at her feet.

A young witch, Chandramukhi, rushed forward.

“Mother, what happened?”

Mahakaali stared at the broken pieces.

“He has touched the royal bloodline.”

“Who?”

The queen closed her eyes.

“Vijay.”

Chandramukhi stepped backward.

“But he is dead!”

Mahakaali looked at her.

“Then why am I afraid?”

Far away, in the Black Forest, every werewolf suddenly stopped moving.

Lord Bhairava rose from his stone throne.

The ground trembled beneath his feet.

An ancient scar across his chest began to bleed.

A young warrior asked, “My lord, what is happening?”

Bhairava looked toward the distant village.

“The child has returned.”

“Should we attack him?”

Bhairava struck the stone wall with his fist.

The wall cracked.

“Attack him?”

He turned toward the warrior.

“Three kingdoms tried to kill him before he could walk.”

His voice dropped.

“And now he walks into a vampire palace alone.”

Bhairava looked toward the mountains.

“Call every pack. Wake the ancient ones. No wolf is to leave the forest alone.”

The warrior bowed.

“Are we going to war?”

Bhairava stared into the darkness.

“No.”

He paused.

“We are preparing to survive one.”

That night, an elderly witch named Shakuni entered an abandoned temple at the edge of the village.

She had served the witches for centuries.

She had also helped write the prophecy that had brought the three kingdoms together twenty years ago.

Someone was waiting for her.

Vijay stood before a stone altar.

“You are late,” he said.

Shakuni lowered her head.

“I had to make sure nobody followed me.”

Vijay turned.

“Did you find my mother?”

“I found something better.”

He said nothing.

Shakuni placed an old piece of cloth on the altar.

It was burned around the edges. In the center was a symbol: three circles joined by a drop of blood.

Vijay recognized it.

He had seen the symbol carved into the door of his childhood home.

“What is this?”

“The mark of the original blood pact.”

“Explain.”

Shakuni took a deep breath.

“The three kingdoms did not create the pact to kill you.”

Vijay's expression hardened.

“Then why?”

“To hide what happened before you were born.”

Shakuni explained that the vampire king, the werewolf lord, and the witch queen had not always been enemies. Long ago, they had served a fourth kingdom that had disappeared from history.

A kingdom ruled by creatures who could command all three bloodlines.

The three circles represented its royal family.

And the blood drop represented its last heir.

Vijay looked at the symbol again.

“Why are you showing me this?”

Shakuni hesitated.

“Because your father was not killed in the massacre.”

Vijay slowly lifted his eyes.

“What did you say?”

“Your father was alive when the palace burned. Someone took him away.”

“Who?”

Shakuni looked terrified.

“I don't know.”

Vijay grabbed the altar.

The stone cracked beneath his fingers.

“You have spent twenty years hiding this from me.”

“I was trying to protect you.”

Vijay laughed bitterly.

“Everyone says that after they betray me.”

He stepped closer.

“Listen carefully, Shakuni. If you are lying, I will find out. If you are telling the truth, I will find out who took my father.”

He turned toward the doorway.

“And if anyone stands between me and the truth…”

His eyes burned red.

“They will learn why three kingdoms were afraid of one child.”

Shakuni watched him leave.

Then she whispered something Vijay could not hear.

“Forgive me, child.”

She opened her hand.

Inside it was a second symbol.

It was identical to the one she had shown Vijay.

Except this one had four circles.

And the fourth circle was already filled with blood.

Deep beneath the vampire palace, a hidden chamber opened.

Three figures entered.

One vampire.

One witch.

One werewolf.

They were the secret leaders of an ancient alliance known as the Council of Shadows.

They had secretly controlled wars, assassinations, and royal marriages for centuries.

The vampire spoke first.

“Vijay has returned.”

The witch nodded.

“The prophecy is moving faster than we expected.”

The werewolf slammed his fist against the table.

“Then we kill him now.”

The vampire shook his head.

“We have tried.”

“Then we try harder!”

The witch looked at both of them.

“You still don't understand. Vijay is not the final threat.”

Silence filled the chamber.

The vampire asked, “What do you mean?”

The witch placed a black stone on the table.

It began to glow.

A voice emerged from inside it.

“Has the boy found out about his father?”

The three leaders immediately knelt.

The voice continued.

“Good. Let him search.”

The werewolf raised his head.

“Master, what if he discovers the fourth kingdom?”

A quiet laugh echoed through the chamber.

“Then he will do exactly what we want.”

The stone went dark.

The three leaders rose slowly.

The vampire looked troubled.

“Who is our master?”

The witch stared at the stone.

“I have served this council for two hundred years.”

She swallowed.

“I have never seen his face.”

Far beyond the three kingdoms, a fortress stood inside a wasteland where even the sun appeared pale.

Inside its highest tower, a man watched the blood moon through a narrow window.

He wore a golden mask.

A messenger entered and knelt.

“Master, Vijay has returned. The vampire king has failed to stop him. The witch queen is preparing an alliance. The werewolf lord is gathering his army.”

The masked man said nothing.

The messenger continued.

“Should we intervene?”

The man slowly removed his mask.

His face was older, scarred, and unmistakably familiar.

It was Vijay's face.

The same red eyes.

The same golden light.

But unlike the young Vijay, this man did not smile.

He looked at a portrait standing beside the window.

A woman.

Vijay's mother.

The older man touched the portrait.

“Not yet.”

The messenger hesitated.

“Master, if the young Vijay becomes stronger than you…”

The older man turned.

For a moment, the air itself seemed to freeze.

“If?”

He stepped toward the messenger.

“You still believe he is becoming stronger than me?”

The messenger lowered his head.

The older man smiled.

“He was born stronger than me.”

He looked back toward the blood moon.

“And that is exactly why I must make him hate the world.”

He placed the golden mask back on his face.

“Let the three kingdoms hunt him.”

Then he gave his final order.

“Make sure they discover the truth about his mother.”

The messenger bowed and left.

The masked man remained alone.

After a long silence, he whispered:

“Forgive me, Vijay.”

A tear rolled down his face.

Then his expression changed.

“But if you become what I became…”

He looked toward the distant village.

“I will be the one who kills you.”

At the edge of Rudhiravan, Vijay stood beneath the blood moon.

Behind him lay the vampire kingdom.

Beyond the mountains waited the werewolves.

Inside the village, the witches were preparing their spells.

All three kingdoms were beginning to move against him.

Vijay knew it.

He could hear their heartbeats. He could feel their magic. He could sense the fear spreading across the land.

He opened his hand.

A dark flame appeared in his palm.

Red at the center.

Golden at the edges.

It was neither vampire magic nor werewolf power nor witchcraft.

It was something older.

Something the world had tried to erase.

Vijay stared at the flame.

For a moment, he remembered his childhood home.

His mother's voice.

His father's hands.

The screams of the night everything had been taken from him.

Then the memories disappeared beneath his anger.

“They took my family.”

The flame grew larger.

“They stole my childhood.”

The ground cracked beneath his feet.

“They made me into the monster they feared.”

The sky darkened.

Vijay raised his eyes toward the three kingdoms.

“Now they will learn the difference between creating a monster…”

He closed his fist.

The flame vanished.

“…and surviving one.”

Far beneath the earth, something ancient opened its eyes.

A voice echoed through the darkness.

“The first blood has returned.”

And for the first time in a thousand years, the ancient seal protecting the fourth kingdom began to break.

Vijay turned toward the forest.

He smiled.

“Come find me.”

END OF EPISODE 1

The first blood has returned. The seal of the fourth kingdom is breaking.
