The Demon Who Walks in Silence

Episode 2 · The Road After Dark

The figure stood beside the old well, staring towards the village. The Agora's ritual had disturbed it. For a long moment, the forest remained completely silent. Then the figure slowly turned away from the well and began walking towards the road that connected the village to the nearby town. Its anger seemed to travel with it.

That night, anyone who used the road after dark never reached their destination. A man riding home on his bicycle heard footsteps behind him. He turned, but the road was empty. He began pedalling faster. The footsteps followed. When he looked back again, a dark shape was already beside him. His bicycle lay abandoned on the roadside the next morning.

A small goods vehicle stopped when its driver noticed something standing in the middle of the road. He stepped out to check. The headlights flickered once. Then the darkness swallowed the road. By morning, the vehicle was found with its doors hanging open. There was no sign of the driver.

One after another, travellers disappeared. Some were found far from the road, while others were never found at all. Nobody could explain what had attacked them. Those who had seen something could not describe it clearly. They remembered only the sudden silence, a shadow, and the feeling that something had been standing right behind them.

Late that night · The road from town to the village

A newly married couple was returning to their village on a motorcycle. They had spent the evening in town. The husband was riding, while his wife sat behind him, holding his shoulders. They were laughing and talking as the motorcycle moved along the quiet road. Neither of them knew what had been happening there. Neither of them knew that the village had been warned not to leave after sunset.

Suddenly, the motorcycle gave a jerk and stopped. “What happened?” his wife asked. “I don't know. Wait a minute.” He tried the ignition again. CLICK. CLICK. CLICK. The engine refused to start.

He looked around. The road was empty. On one side stood a thick forest, and on the other was a stretch of darkness with no houses or streetlights. “There's nothing here. I'll walk towards the town and get some help. You wait beside the bike. Don't go anywhere.” “Are you sure? It's very dark.” “I'll be back soon. Just stay here.”

He started walking towards the town, leaving his wife beside the motorcycle. She watched him disappear into the darkness. At first, she tried to stay calm. She checked her phone, but there was barely any network. She looked towards the road, hoping to see another vehicle. Nothing.

Then she heard a branch snap inside the forest. She turned quickly. There was nobody there. A few leaves shook above her. Another branch fell near the roadside. She took a step closer to the motorcycle. Something seemed to be watching her from between the trees.

She looked towards the forest again. A shadow moved behind a tree. “Hello? Who's there?” No answer. The branches began moving around her, one after another. The leaves rustled, though there was no wind. She felt that whatever was in the forest was getting closer. Closer. And closer.

Suddenly, someone jumped at her from behind. “AAAAAHHHHH!” She screamed at the top of her voice and stumbled backwards. Then she heard laughter. It was her husband. “You should have seen your face!” he laughed.

She stared at him, furious. “Are you mad? I was already frightened! Why would you do that?” “Sorry, sorry!” he said, still smiling. He hid one hand behind his back. Then he brought out a small bunch of beautiful flowers he had picked from the roadside forest.

He stood in front of her. “I know I scared you. But I wanted to make this night special. I love you.” Her anger slowly disappeared. She looked at the flowers and then at him. “I love you too, idiot.” They both laughed and hugged. He placed the flowers safely with her, got on the motorcycle and tried the ignition. This time, the engine started immediately. “See? Nothing is wrong with my bike,” he joked. “Just ride. It's getting late.”

A little later · The same road

The motorcycle travelled through another stretch of forest. Suddenly, the engine sputtered and stopped again. The wife looked at him. “Really? Again? Stop playing games. It's getting very late. Let's go home.” “I'm not doing anything. The bike has actually stopped.” He tried starting it several times. Nothing happened.

His wife got down and walked a little way along the roadside. “Check it properly. Maybe something is wrong with the petrol.” “I checked that already. Just give me a minute.” She continued talking while he bent over the motorcycle, checking the engine and trying the ignition. “You always say your bike is in perfect condition. Look where it has brought us!” He smiled faintly. “I'll get it fixed tomorrow. Now let me see what's wrong.”

Then, without warning, the forest fell silent. No insects. No birds. No rustling leaves. Not even the faintest movement in the bushes. The wife stopped speaking. Something had appeared behind her. She turned towards it. Her eyes widened. And then she was gone. No scream. No footsteps running away. Nothing.

Her husband was still bent over the motorcycle, concentrating on the engine. He hadn't noticed anything. After a few attempts, the engine finally started. “Finally!” he said, straightening up. He turned around. “Come on, let's go!” There was no answer. He looked towards the place where she had been standing. Empty.

“Hey! Stop hiding. Come on, it's not funny.” He walked a few steps and looked behind the trees. “Where are you?” No response. He called her phone. There was no connection. He tried again. Nothing.

He began to smile nervously. “Okay, very funny. You scared me once. Now come out.” He thought she was playing the same prank he had played on her earlier. He got back on the motorcycle but did not start moving. Instead, he began singing a romantic song they both loved.

He sang the first line. Then he heard something. TAP... TAP... TAP... Footsteps. He stopped singing for a moment. The footsteps stopped too. He smiled. “I know you're there.” He started singing again. The footsteps continued. He was certain she was hiding nearby and walking towards him.

But something felt wrong. The sound was coming from directly in front of him. He continued singing, trying to hide his nervousness. TAP... TAP... TAP... The footsteps were getting closer. Closer. And closer. His smile slowly disappeared.

He stopped singing. The footsteps stopped. He raised his head. Something was standing directly in front of him. A figure. He could not move. He could not speak. He simply stared at the creature standing before him. He stood completely still.

Meanwhile · The village

Back in the village, the couple's parents were waiting anxiously. Both families had been expecting them to arrive much earlier. The hours had passed, but there was still no sign of the couple. Their phones were not connecting because of the poor network in the area.

“Why haven't they reached yet?” the mother asked, looking towards the road. “They should have been here by now,” someone replied. Neighbours had gathered outside their houses. The news of the missing children had already frightened everyone. Now the late arrival of the newly married couple made their fear even worse. The entire village seemed to be waiting.

Then, from far away, a sound travelled along the road. BRRRRRRRRMMMM... A motorcycle. Everyone turned towards the village entrance. Doors opened. People stepped outside. Parents hurried towards the road, hoping it was the couple. But nobody crossed the village boundary. They remembered the Agora's warning: Nobody leaves the village after sunset.

The motorcycle sound grew louder. Its headlight appeared in the distance. “They're coming!” someone shouted. The parents moved forward instinctively. Then the motorcycle reached the village border. The husband suddenly lost control. The bike swerved. CRASH! He fell hard onto the roadside, just outside the village boundary.

“SON!” his father screamed. Several villagers rushed forward. But before anyone could cross the boundary, the Agora stepped in front of them. He raised his staff. “STOP!” His voice cut through the night. “Nobody crosses this line!”

The parents struggled against the villagers holding them back. “That's our son! Let us go!” The Agora did not move. He stared beyond the fallen motorcycle, towards the darkness of the forest road. His face changed. For the first time that night, the Agora looked afraid.
