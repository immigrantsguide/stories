EPISODE 3

The Man Who Returned Alone

The road through the forest was empty. The motorcycle stood beneath a crooked tree, its headlight flickering. Ravi was bent over the engine, trying to understand why it had stopped again. A few steps away, his wife Sruthi waited beside the road, holding the flowers he had given her earlier that night.

“Ravi, can we please leave?” she asked. “I don't like this place.”

“Just one minute, Sruthi. I'll get it started.”

She looked toward the trees. The branches were moving, but there was no wind. A dry leaf landed on her shoulder. Then another fell near her feet.

“Ravi…”

He didn't look up. “Almost done.”

A sound came from deep inside the forest. It was like someone dragging a heavy object over stones. Sruthi stepped closer to the motorcycle.

Then the forest went silent.

Not quiet. Silent. The insects stopped. The leaves stopped moving. Even the engine seemed to fall away from the world.

Sruthi turned slowly. Between two trees, something tall stood watching her.

“Ravi?” she whispered.

The shape moved.

She ran.

“RAVI!”

Ravi looked up at last. He saw Sruthi sprinting toward him, terror twisting her face. Behind her, a dark figure moved between the trees with impossible speed.

“SRUTHI!”

He ran toward her, but the creature reached her first.

KRRRRAAAAAAA—!

Its arm caught her and dragged her off the road. Sruthi clawed at the dirt, grabbing roots and stones. Her fingers slipped. She screamed Ravi's name as the creature pulled her into the darkness.

Ravi froze for one terrible second. Then he charged into the forest.

He found her beneath a tree. The creature crouched over her. Sruthi was still alive, struggling weakly and trying to crawl away. The thing lowered its head toward her. Its mouth opened far wider than any human mouth could open.

“NO! LEAVE HER!” Ravi screamed.

He grabbed a fallen branch and struck the creature's back. The blow made a dull, hollow sound. The creature barely moved. It turned its head toward him, while one claw pinned Sruthi to the ground.

Ravi struck again. “TAKE ME! LET HER GO!”

Sruthi looked at him through tears. “Ravi… run…”

The creature bent toward her again. Ravi watched helplessly as it began to feed. Her cries became weaker, then broke into frightened whispers. The forest swallowed every sound except Ravi's desperate sobbing and the slow, horrible noises coming from the darkness.

He fell to his knees. “Sruthi… please… please…”

When the creature finally lifted its head, Ravi could no longer reach her. He crawled toward her, crying, but a long shadow fell across him.

“You…” Ravi whispered, looking up. “You killed my wife.”

The creature stood over him. Its eyes glowed like two embers buried in a grave.

Ravi tried to rise. The creature struck him down. He hit the ground hard and struggled to breathe. The thing leaned close, studying his face as if memorising every detail.

Then its body began to change.

The long limbs shortened. The hunched back straightened. The claws withdrew. Its face rippled like a reflection disturbed by a stone. Slowly, impossibly, the creature took Ravi's human form.

It looked down at the dead man, then touched its new face.

“Ravi…” it whispered, practising his voice.

It walked back to the motorcycle, lifted it from the ground and turned the key. The engine started at once.

—

In the village, parents waited outside their homes. The couple had not returned. Their phones were unreachable, and the weak network had made every call useless. No one wanted to say aloud what they feared.

At the edge of the village, the Agora stood beneath the old banyan tree. He had ordered everyone to remain behind the boundary after sunset. His trident rested against his shoulder, and sacred ash covered his forehead.

A motorcycle engine approached.

“Ravi has come!” someone cried.

Villagers rushed out of their houses. They hurried toward the road, but stopped at the boundary. The Agora raised his hand.

“Stay where you are.”

The motorcycle emerged from the darkness. Ravi was riding it. He slowed near the boundary and let the bike fall onto its stand.

“Help me!” he cried. “Please!”

His voice sounded exactly like Ravi's. His clothes were torn. His face was covered in mud. He looked exhausted.

An elderly woman tried to run to him. “My child!”

The Agora blocked her path.

“Don't cross.”

“Swami, that's Ravi! Let us bring him inside!”

The Agora stared at the young man. Something about his shadow was wrong. The motorcycle's headlight shone behind him, but his shadow stretched toward the light instead of away from it.

“Where is Sruthi?” the Agora asked.

Ravi's face crumpled. “She… she is gone.”

“What happened?”

“Something took her from the road.”

“Did you see it?”

Ravi nodded. “It was standing in front of me. I couldn't see its face. Only its eyes.”

“What did its eyes look at?”

The young man slowly raised his head.

“Not me.”

He smiled faintly.

“They were looking at someone behind me.”

The villagers turned. There was nobody there.

Then a woman's voice came from the darkness inside the village.

“Ravi…”

The young man began to tremble. “Sruthi?”

The Agora stepped forward. “Do not answer that voice.”

“Ravi… I'm waiting…”

“That's my wife!” Ravi cried, taking a step toward the sound.

The Agora seized his arm. “That is not your wife.”

The young man's expression changed. His grief vanished. His mouth stretched into a smile that was much too wide.

“You told them not to open their doors,” he said. His voice was no longer Ravi's. “But you never told them to keep their temples locked.”

Every oil lamp in the village went out.

THUD.

THUD.

THUD.

The knocks came from the village temple.

An old man stared at the dark doorway. “That temple has been locked for twenty years…”

The Agora lifted his trident. “Everyone behind me.”

The young man laughed. His skin shivered. His shoulders rose and twisted. The face of Ravi began to slide away, revealing the terrible shape underneath.

“You came to my land,” the Agora said. “You took the innocent. You crossed the boundary. Tonight, you will not leave.”

The creature answered with a shriek that rattled the windows.

KRRRRAAAAAAAAAAAAA!

It lunged.

The Agora swung his trident. A burst of sparks exploded as metal struck the creature's claws. The impact threw dust across the road. The villagers screamed and stumbled backward.

The creature sprang onto the banyan tree, raced along a thick branch and dropped behind the Agora. Its claws slashed across his shoulder. The Agora staggered, but turned and drove the trident into the ground.

He began chanting. The ash markings on his chest glowed faintly. A ring of fire raced across the earth, forming a circle around the creature.

For a moment, the demon was trapped.

Then it smiled.

It pressed both hands against the invisible barrier. The ground cracked. The fire bent inward, as though something beneath the soil were sucking it away.

“Impossible,” the Agora muttered.

The creature burst through the circle and hurled itself at him. They crashed into the banyan tree. The Agora struck its face with the base of his trident. The creature answered with a blow that sent him rolling across the dirt.

He rose, blood running from his forehead. He drew a symbol in the air and shouted a sacred command. The wind returned in a violent rush. Doors slammed. Roof tiles rattled. The creature was dragged backward, its feet carving two deep lines in the earth.

“You should have stayed beneath the well!” the Agora roared.

The creature's head snapped toward him.

“THE WELL WAS ONLY A DOOR.”

It struck the ground. A shockwave shattered the circle of ash. The Agora was thrown against the temple steps. His trident skidded away.

The creature advanced. Behind it, the temple doors began to shake again.

SCRRRRRRRRR…

The Agora forced himself upright and reached for his trident. The creature leapt. He caught it in midair with the shaft and drove it into the stone floor. The two struggled, face to face. The demon's claws pressed against his throat. The Agora's arms trembled as he held it back.

“You are not invincible,” he gasped.

The creature leaned close. “Neither are you.”

With a sudden twist, it flung him aside and sprinted toward the forest.

The Agora slammed his trident into the earth. A column of fire erupted across the creature's path. The demon recoiled, its skin smoking where the flames touched it. It screamed and stumbled back.

For one brief moment, the Agora had it.

He raised both hands and began the final chant. The markings on his chest burned brighter. The air thickened. The creature's limbs locked in place.

Then the temple bell rang by itself.

GONGGGGGGG…

The Agora faltered. His eyes darted toward the temple.

That single moment was enough.

The creature tore free, leapt over the wall of flame and ran into the forest. Its shape changed as it moved—first a man, then a twisted shadow, then something too tall to be human. The trees swallowed it.

The Agora hurled his trident after it. The weapon struck a tree trunk and buried itself in the wood.

Silence returned.

The villagers slowly emerged from their homes. The Agora stood breathing heavily, one hand pressed to his wounded shoulder. He looked toward the forest, then toward the temple.

“It has escaped,” an old man whispered.

The Agora pulled his trident free from the tree.

“No,” he said. “It has only gone back to prepare.”

From deep inside the forest came a faint sound.

“Shhhhh…”

The Agora's face turned pale.

Because this time, the voice came from behind him.

END OF EPISODE 3
