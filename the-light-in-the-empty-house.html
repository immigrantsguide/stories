THE LIGHT IN THE EMPTY HOUSE

Episode 2: The Village That Feared the Dead

[ ORIGINAL STORY ]

Horror • Supernatural Mystery • Psychological Thriller

Created by Vijay Krishna

At 2:17 a.m., the entire village of Rudrapuram woke to the sound of a woman screaming.

It came from the abandoned house at the end of the village.

The same house that had been locked for twenty years.

The same house where Raghava, Lakshmi, Ananya, and Chinnu had once lived happily.

The same house whose upstairs bedroom mysteriously illuminated itself every night, despite having no electricity.

That night, every dog in Rudrapuram howled at once.

Doors slammed shut.

Windows rattled.

And across the village, people felt the temperature suddenly collapse.

Inside their homes, villagers watched their breath turn white.

A farmer named Narasimha sat upright in bed, shivering beneath two blankets. His wife grabbed his arm.

"Do you hear that?"

From somewhere beyond their bedroom wall came the sound of a little boy laughing.

Narasimha stopped breathing for a moment.

His wife began trembling.

Their house was nearly half a kilometer from the abandoned property.

Yet the laughter sounded as though a child were standing directly behind their bed.

Then came three knocks.

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

Narasimha slowly turned toward the bedroom door.

Nobody was there.

But beneath the door, something dark began spreading across the floor.

It looked like water.

Then it started moving against gravity, climbing the wooden door in thin, twisting streams.

His wife screamed.

The lights went out.

And a small voice whispered from the darkness.

"Uncle... have you seen my sister?"

By morning, Narasimha was dead.

The doctors could find no obvious explanation for his sudden death. His wife survived, but she refused to speak. Whenever anyone mentioned the abandoned house, she covered her ears and began shaking violently.

On the same morning, a second tragedy struck Rudrapuram.

A shopkeeper who had publicly laughed at the village's ghost stories was found unconscious inside his locked shop. Every bottle on his shelves had shattered. The walls were covered in black fingerprints, and the entire room smelled of rotten eggs and stale alcohol.

When he regained consciousness, he had only one sentence to say.

"Two people came to my shop last night."

"Who?" the police officer asked.

The shopkeeper stared at the floor.

"A young woman... and a man."

"Did you recognize them?"

His lips trembled.

"The woman was crying."

He swallowed hard.

"The man was laughing."

"Who were they?"

The shopkeeper suddenly grabbed the officer's wrist.

"They had no shadows."

Then he began screaming.

Across the village, another family discovered that their front door had been scratched from the outside.

Hundreds of marks covered the wood.

All of them pointed inward.

As though someone had been desperately trying to escape from the other side.

By noon, the village elders held an emergency meeting.

Nobody mentioned the house by name.

Nobody wanted to.

Finally, the oldest man in Rudrapuram stood up.

His hands trembled against his walking stick.

"Listen carefully," he warned. "From today onward, nobody speaks about those two houses after sunset."

A young villager laughed nervously.

"Why? Will the ghosts hear us?"

The old man slowly turned toward him.

"No."

His expression changed.

"They already know what you're going to say."

That evening, the young villager disappeared.

His sandals were found outside the abandoned house.

The gate was still locked.

And from the upstairs bedroom came the sound of someone dragging a chair across the floor.

Three days later, a black SUV entered Rudrapuram.

A young man named Arjun stepped out, carrying a backpack and a small camera. He had come to the village after discovering an old newspaper clipping about a missing daughter, an unidentified disappearance, and a family that had died under mysterious circumstances.

The article was twenty years old.

Its headline read:

FAMILY TRAGEDY REMAINS UNSOLVED — DAUGHTER AND NEIGHBOR STILL MISSING.

Arjun had always been fascinated by unexplained cases.

But the moment he stepped into Rudrapuram, he felt something he could not explain.

The air was unusually cold.

Not a pleasant winter chill.

It was the kind of cold that entered the bones and made the body instinctively prepare for danger.

He looked at the temperature displayed on his phone.

Thirty-nine degrees Celsius.

Yet his fingers were numb.

Across the road, an elderly woman was drawing water from a hand pump.

Arjun approached her.

"Excuse me, madam. I'm looking for an old house near the end of the village. A family named Raghava used to live there."

The woman dropped her metal pot.

It struck the ground with a deafening clang.

She stared at him.

"Who told you that name?"

"I read about the family. I wanted to learn what happened."

The woman's face went pale.

She grabbed her pot and hurried away.

Arjun followed her with his eyes.

He approached a tea stall.

"Anna, can you tell me where Raghava's house is?"

The shopkeeper stopped pouring tea.

A man sitting on a wooden bench immediately stood up and left.

Another customer threw money onto the counter and ran toward the road.

The shopkeeper leaned closer.

"Don't ask that question here."

"Why?"

"Because you're new."

Arjun smiled uneasily.

"I'm just doing some research."

The shopkeeper's eyes moved toward the northern end of the village.

"Then research something else."

"Did the family die there?"

The shopkeeper grabbed Arjun's wrist.

"Listen to me. If you want to leave this village alive, forget that house exists."

Arjun pulled his hand away.

"Are you telling me there's a ghost?"

The shopkeeper looked over his shoulder.

For a moment, his face became strangely blank.

Then he whispered:

"Ghosts are not the worst things that live there."

A glass fell from the shelf behind him.

It shattered.

Every person inside the tea stall turned toward the sound.

In the broken pieces, Arjun noticed something that made his stomach tighten.

A reflection.

A young woman stood behind him.

Her face was covered by long black hair.

Her clothes were soaked.

Arjun turned around.

Nobody was there.

When he looked back at the glass, the reflection had disappeared.

The shopkeeper began trembling.

"Go away," he whispered. "Please. Just go away."

Arjun walked out.

His curiosity had become stronger than his fear.

Someone in Rudrapuram knew the truth.

And someone was determined to keep it buried.

By sunset, Arjun had questioned seven villagers.

All seven had refused to answer.

One man crossed himself and walked away.

Another began reciting prayers.

An elderly farmer slammed his door so violently that a framed photograph fell from the wall inside.

Finally, Arjun found an old man sitting beneath a massive banyan tree near the village temple.

His name was Veeranna.

He was perhaps eighty years old, with a deeply lined face, cloudy eyes, and a walking stick carved from dark wood.

Unlike everyone else, Veeranna did not run when Arjun mentioned Raghava.

Instead, he studied the young man for a long time.

"Why do you want to know about that family?"

"Because nobody will tell me anything."

"That should be enough to make you leave."

"It isn't."

Veeranna lowered his head.

For several seconds, neither man spoke.

Then the old man asked a question.

"Do you believe the dead can recognize the living?"

Arjun frowned.

"I don't know."

"Good."

Veeranna stood up slowly.

"Because if you believe they cannot, you may walk into a place from which even the living cannot bring you back."

He began walking toward a narrow path.

"Come with me. But promise me something."

"What?"

"If you hear someone calling your name tonight, don't answer."

Arjun followed him.

As they approached the abandoned properties, the village sounds began to disappear.

The crickets stopped.

The dogs fell silent.

Even the wind seemed to avoid the road.

Then Arjun saw them.

Two houses.

Standing side by side.

One had a broken front gate and a dead garden.

The other was darker, its windows covered with thick wooden boards.

The house where Raghava's family had lived.

And the house where Ravi had once lived with his mother, Savitri.

Arjun stopped walking.

A terrible smell drifted across the road.

Sulfur.

Rotten earth.

Stale liquor.

Something else, something damp and ancient, seemed to cling to the air.

He pulled his shirt over his nose.

"What's that smell?"

Veeranna did not answer.

Arjun stepped closer to the first gate.

A sudden shiver ran down his spine.

The metal bars were covered in frost.

He touched one.

It was painfully cold.

He jerked his hand away.

"How is that possible? It's almost forty degrees outside."

Veeranna looked at him.

"Because the houses don't belong to the weather anymore."

Arjun stared at him.

"What does that mean?"

The old man turned toward the upstairs window.

A yellow light flickered behind the glass.

"Twenty years ago," he began, "a family lived there."

He paused.

"And before the daughter disappeared, something happened that nobody in this village has ever dared to explain."

Veeranna told Arjun that Raghava had once been among the most respected men in Rudrapuram.

He worked hard, loved his wife Lakshmi, and would do anything to protect his children.

Ananya, his daughter, was courageous and independent.

Chinnu, his younger son, lived with a mental illness. He sometimes struggled to understand danger, but he found joy in simple things, especially butterflies.

Lakshmi cared for both children with endless patience.

Their home had been filled with the smell of fresh food, the sound of family conversations, and the laughter of children.

On the front wall hung their favorite photograph.

Raghava stood beside Lakshmi.

Ananya held Chinnu's hand.

All four were smiling.

"They were good people," Veeranna said. "They did not deserve what happened."

Arjun glanced at the house.

"What happened to them?"

The old man's hands tightened around his walking stick.

"Ravi."

The name seemed to change the air around them.

A branch snapped somewhere inside the neighboring garden.

Arjun turned sharply.

Nobody was there.

Veeranna continued.

"Ravi was Savitri's only son. He drank, used drugs, threatened people, and behaved as if he had no fear of consequences. He harassed women in the market and deliberately humiliated anyone who challenged him."

"Was he dangerous?"

"Very."

The old man looked toward the boarded windows of Ravi's house.

"But what frightened us most was not what he did in public."

"What was it?"

"He knew things he had no way of knowing."

Veeranna described how Ravi would sometimes approach strangers and tell them private details about their families.

He knew when people were alone.

He knew which doors were unlocked.

He knew when someone was grieving.

And sometimes, he would stare at an empty corner and begin laughing as though someone had told him a joke.

One evening, a woman selling vegetables asked him why he was staring at the empty space beside her.

Ravi smiled.

"I'm not staring at the empty space."

He pointed behind her.

"I'm looking at the person standing there."

The woman turned.

Nobody was there.

When she looked back, Ravi had vanished.

That night, the woman heard knocking on her bedroom window.

Three times.

When she opened the curtains, she saw Ravi standing outside.

Except he was not looking at her.

He was looking over her shoulder.

The woman screamed and ran to her husband.

By the time they returned, the road was empty.

But muddy footprints led from the window to the bedroom door.

And the footprints were pointing inward.

Arjun felt the hair rise on his arms.

"Are you saying Ravi was possessed?"

Veeranna stopped walking.

"No."

His voice dropped to a whisper.

"I'm saying we never knew who Ravi really was."

The old man continued the story.

After Ravi was arrested for harassing Ananya and threatening the family, the village expected peace.

For several days, there was none of the shouting that had become familiar.

No bottles smashed against walls.

No drunken arguments.

No footsteps outside the neighboring windows.

But the silence was worse.

On the afternoon Chinnu disappeared, Lakshmi was preparing dinner when she noticed her son was missing.

Raghava searched the house.

Ananya checked the garden.

Then the entire family began searching the village.

At midnight, they were still calling his name.

And from somewhere inside Ravi's locked house came laughter.

Veeranna stopped speaking.

Arjun waited.

"Who was laughing?"

The old man stared at him.

"That's what we thought we knew."

The next morning, Savitri went into her backyard to clean near the old well.

She saw something below.

A small body.

Chinnu had fallen into the well and died.

The village mourned him.

But the death changed everything.

Lakshmi began hearing her son asking for food.

Raghava became convinced that something was following him.

Ananya disappeared.

Ravi vanished.

And Savitri abandoned her home.

Within months, Lakshmi died by suicide, Raghava died in a road accident, and Ananya was never found.

The authorities investigated Chinnu's death and searched for Ravi and Ananya.

No conclusive explanation emerged.

The cases remained unresolved.

Veeranna's eyes filled with tears.

"Do you know what is the most frightening thing about that family?"

Arjun shook his head.

"Nobody ever proved that Ravi killed Chinnu."

"But everyone suspected him."

"Yes."

"Then what else could have happened?"

Veeranna looked toward the upstairs window.

"Perhaps nothing."

He paused.

"Or perhaps the boy who fell into the well was not the only one who died that night."

Arjun's heart seemed to stop.

"What are you talking about?"

Veeranna turned away.

"That is the question nobody wants to answer."

Arjun asked about Ananya.

For the first time, Veeranna looked genuinely frightened.

"Nobody knows where she went."

"Didn't anyone search for her?"

"The police searched. Her relatives searched. We searched the fields, the railway station, the bus stand, and every village nearby."

"Was she ever seen again?"

Veeranna did not answer immediately.

Then he said, "The lorry drivers saw her."

Arjun leaned forward.

"What did they see?"

"A young woman standing on the highway, asking for a ride."

Veeranna described the first report.

A lorry driver had been traveling toward the city when he saw a woman in a pale dress beside the road.

She raised her hand.

He stopped.

"Are you hurt?" he asked.

"Please take me home," she replied.

"Where do you live?"

She pointed toward Rudrapuram.

The driver told police that she climbed into the cabin.

He drove for nearly fifteen minutes.

Then he noticed something strange.

The woman was staring at the rear-view mirror.

Not at the road.

Not at him.

At the mirror.

"What are you looking at?" he asked.

She whispered, "He's following us."

The driver checked the mirror.

The road behind them was empty.

When he turned back, the passenger seat was empty too.

He slammed on the brakes.

The truck stopped in the middle of the highway.

He searched the cabin, checked the doors, and looked beneath the vehicle.

There was no sign of the woman.

But the passenger seat was covered in water.

And on the dashboard lay a photograph.

It showed Raghava's family standing outside their house.

Someone had scratched Ananya's face out.

The driver brought the photograph to the police.

Before the officers could question him further, he began screaming that a woman was standing behind them.

The officers turned.

Nobody was there.

Then every light in the station went out.

When the lights returned, the photograph was missing.

So was the driver.

He was found the next morning, sitting beneath a bridge several kilometers away, unable to remember how he had arrived there.

He refused to drive a lorry again.

Arjun swallowed.

"How many drivers saw her?"

"Seven that we know of."

"And Ravi?"

Veeranna's expression hardened.

"Three drivers claimed they saw a man walking behind her."

"Was it Ravi?"

"Every one of them described the same face."

"Then why didn't the police find him?"

The old man looked directly into Arjun's eyes.

"Because every time someone got close enough to identify him, the man disappeared."

Arjun felt a sudden chill.

"Disappeared how?"

Veeranna leaned closer.

"One driver said Ravi was standing beside the road. The next moment, he was standing inside the truck."

"That is impossible."

"Another driver said he saw Ravi sitting in the back of his vehicle. When he checked, there was nobody there."

Veeranna's voice trembled.

"And the third driver said he had been driving for an hour with Ravi sitting beside him, smiling."

"What happened?"

"The driver looked away for a second."

Veeranna paused.

"When he looked back, Ravi was still there."

"Then?"

"The driver's hair had turned white."

Arjun stared at him.

"That can't be true."

Veeranna pointed toward the two houses.

"Then go and ask him yourself."

By then, the sky had darkened.

Arjun looked at his phone.

There was no signal.

He tried calling his hotel.

Nothing.

He opened his map.

The screen flickered, and the village road disappeared.

In its place appeared a black screen with four words.

DON'T LET HER IN.

Arjun dropped the phone.

It struck the road and went dark.

"Did you see that?" he asked.

Veeranna was staring at the neighboring house.

The upstairs window had opened.

Slowly.

A pale hand emerged from the darkness.

It gripped the window frame.

Then another hand appeared.

Someone was climbing out.

Arjun stepped backward.

A young man stood at the window.

His face was thin, his hair disheveled, and his shirt stained with dark patches.

He looked exactly like the description of Ravi in the old newspaper.

The man smiled.

Then he raised one hand and waved at Arjun.

Veeranna dropped his walking stick.

"Impossible," he whispered.

Arjun turned toward him.

"What is it?"

The old man's face had lost all color.

"Ravi was never seen again after the night Chinnu died."

"But he's standing right there!"

Veeranna grabbed Arjun's arm.

"Run."

The man at the window laughed.

His mouth opened wider than it should have.

The sound that emerged did not resemble ordinary laughter.

It sounded like several people laughing together, all at different distances.

Then the window slammed shut.

Every light in both houses turned on simultaneously.

Arjun heard footsteps inside the first house.

A child running down the stairs.

A woman sobbing.

A man shouting.

Then a heavy crash.

Veeranna began shaking uncontrollably.

"Don't look at the windows," he whispered.

"Why?"

"Because if they see you looking, they'll know you can see them."

Arjun turned away.

But he had already seen something else.

Behind the upstairs curtain of Raghava's house stood a young woman.

Ananya.

She was pressing both hands against the glass.

Her eyes were wide with terror.

Her lips moved.

Arjun could not hear her voice.

But he understood the words.

HELP ME.

Then another face appeared behind her.

A young man.

Ravi.

He leaned close to her ear.

Ananya began screaming.

The window went black.

Arjun ran.

Veeranna followed him as quickly as his old legs would allow.

Behind them, the gates of both houses began opening by themselves.

Metal scraped against metal.

The air grew colder.

And from inside the darkness came a voice that followed them down the road.

"Arjun..."

He stopped.

Veeranna grabbed him.

"Don't answer!"

The voice came again.

This time, it sounded like his mother.

"Arjun, come back."

His heart dropped.

He turned instinctively.

At the far end of the road stood a woman wearing a pale dress.

Her face was hidden.

Her arms hung at her sides.

She tilted her head.

Then she lifted one hand and pointed toward the abandoned house.

Arjun could not move.

Veeranna dragged him away.

"That's not your mother!"

The woman began walking toward them.

Slowly.

One step at a time.

Her feet made no sound against the road.

When she reached the streetlight, the bulb exploded.

Darkness swallowed the road.

Something cold brushed Arjun's cheek.

He felt breath against his ear.

And a woman whispered:

"You shouldn't have come here."

They reached the banyan tree just before midnight.

Veeranna collapsed onto the stone bench, gasping for breath.

Arjun could barely speak.

"Who was that woman?"

The old man covered his face.

"I don't know."

"Was it Ananya?"

"I don't know!"

"Then why are you hiding the truth from me?"

Veeranna slowly lowered his hands.

For a long time, he stared at the ground.

Then he began to cry.

"Because I was there that night."

Arjun froze.

"What?"

"The night Chinnu died."

The old man told him that, on the evening of Chinnu's disappearance, he had been walking past the neighboring house when he heard an argument.

He had seen a young man running through the backyard.

A few moments later, he had heard a scream near the well.

He had hurried toward the sound.

But when he reached the backyard, there was nobody there.

The well was silent.

The garden was empty.

Then he noticed a small object lying in the dirt.

A butterfly.

Its wings were wet.

He picked it up.

From inside the neighboring house, someone began laughing.

Veeranna looked toward the window.

He saw a shadow move across the wall.

Then another.

Then a third.

He heard a woman screaming.

He ran away.

He never told the police what he had heard because he feared that whoever was inside the house would come after him.

For twenty years, he had kept the secret.

Now, as he finished speaking, Arjun noticed that the old man's hands were trembling more violently than before.

"Why tell me now?"

Veeranna looked up.

"Because I saw you standing at that window."

"And?"

"You weren't alone."

Arjun felt his throat tighten.

"What do you mean?"

Veeranna leaned forward.

"When you saw Ananya, I saw someone standing behind you."

"Ravi?"

The old man shook his head.

"No."

"Then who?"

Veeranna's voice became almost inaudible.

"The boy who died in the well."

Arjun could not breathe.

"Chinnu?"

The old man nodded.

"He was holding your hand."

Arjun looked down at his right hand.

There was a muddy fingerprint on his wrist.

It was small.

Like the hand of a child.

And beneath the fingerprint, the skin had turned a deep blue.

A sudden burst of cold air swept beneath the banyan tree.

Veeranna looked toward the village.

The clock in the temple struck midnight.

Then his face changed.

He stared at Arjun with an expression of pure terror.

"What?" Arjun demanded.

Veeranna pointed toward the road.

At the far end of the street stood two figures.

One was a young woman in a pale dress.

The other was a young man with a twisted smile.

Between them stood a small boy.

The three figures were holding hands.

The woman looked toward Arjun.

The boy raised his head.

And Ravi began laughing.

Veeranna whispered a prayer.

Then the old man disappeared.

Not ran away.

Not walked away.

He simply vanished from the stone bench, leaving his walking stick behind.

Arjun stood alone beneath the banyan tree.

The three figures were gone.

But the cold remained.

From somewhere inside the abandoned house, a little boy began calling.

"Arjun..."

He covered his ears.

The voice grew louder.

"Arjun, I'm hungry."

The yellow light appeared upstairs.

Then the front door opened.

And from inside the house, a woman's voice whispered:

"Come inside, Arjun."

Arjun should have left Rudrapuram.

He knew it.

Every instinct in his body was screaming at him to get into his vehicle and drive as far away as possible.

But Veeranna had disappeared.

The village had hidden the truth for twenty years.

And now Arjun had seen Ananya.

Or something pretending to be her.

He returned to his SUV and tried to start the engine.

It would not turn over.

He tried again.

Nothing.

Then he heard a soft tapping on the passenger window.

A little boy stood outside.

Chinnu.

His clothes were soaked.

Water dripped from his hair.

He looked up at Arjun and smiled sadly.

"Uncle, can you take me home?"

Arjun shut his eyes.

When he opened them, the boy was gone.

But the passenger door was unlocked.

Something was sitting inside the vehicle.

A wet butterfly rested on the seat.

Arjun stepped out and ran toward the main road.

As he passed the abandoned house, he heard a loud crash from upstairs.

The front door swung open.

Inside, the hallway was illuminated by a yellow lamp.

On the wall hung the family photograph.

Arjun stopped.

The picture was no longer the same.

Raghava and Lakshmi stood beside their children.

Ananya was staring directly at the camera.

Chinnu was holding a butterfly.

Behind them stood Ravi.

But another person had appeared at the edge of the frame.

Arjun.

He was standing outside the house, wearing the same clothes he had on that night.

His face was pale.

His right hand was covered in mud.

And someone was gripping his shoulder from behind.

Arjun moved closer to the photograph.

The person behind him had no face.

Suddenly, the picture began to change.

Raghava disappeared.

Then Lakshmi.

Then Chinnu.

One by one, the faces vanished until only Ananya and Ravi remained.

Ananya was crying.

Ravi was smiling.

Then a line appeared across the glass, as though someone were scratching it from the other side.

ONE OF THEM IS STILL ALIVE.

Arjun staggered backward.

The lamp flickered.

From upstairs came the sound of footsteps.

A door opened.

Then someone began descending the staircase.

Arjun ran toward the front gate.

But the gate had closed.

He pulled at it.

It would not move.

Behind him, the footsteps stopped.

A voice whispered:

"Arjun."

He turned.

At the top of the stairs stood Ananya.

Her face was pale, her hair hung over her shoulders, and her dress was covered in mud.

She looked directly at him.

"Please," she whispered. "You have to help me."

Arjun stepped toward her.

"What happened to you?"

Her eyes filled with tears.

"I don't remember."

"Where is Ravi?"

Ananya's face changed.

The yellow light began flickering faster.

Her lips trembled.

Then she whispered something that made Arjun's blood run cold.

"Ravi is not the one you should be afraid of."

A loud crash came from the room behind her.

Ananya screamed.

Someone grabbed her from the darkness.

She vanished.

The bedroom door slammed shut.

And from inside the locked room came two voices.

One was Ravi's.

The other was Arjun's.

Both were laughing.

At dawn, the villagers found Arjun sitting beside the banyan tree.

His SUV stood on the road.

The engine was running.

The passenger door was open.

Arjun could not remember how he had escaped the house.

He could not remember where Veeranna had gone.

And he could not explain the muddy fingerprint on his wrist.

The police searched both properties.

They found no sign of Ananya.

No sign of Ravi.

No sign of Chinnu.

But inside the upstairs bedroom of Raghava's house, they discovered a new photograph.

It showed the four members of the family.

Raghava.

Lakshmi.

Ananya.

Chinnu.

Behind them stood Ravi.

And in front of them stood Arjun.

The police officer examining the photograph noticed something strange.

Arjun's eyes were closed.

His hands were folded.

And a thin line of water ran from his mouth to the bottom of the frame.

The officer dropped the photograph.

"Who took this?"

Nobody answered.

Then a constable came running from the neighboring house.

"Sir! You need to see this!"

The officers hurried outside.

A fresh set of footprints had appeared in the dirt between the two properties.

One set belonged to a child.

One set belonged to a woman.

One set belonged to a man.

All three sets led toward the old well.

But none led away.

The police searched the well.

They found nothing.

That evening, Arjun left Rudrapuram.

He never returned.

For several weeks, he told nobody what he had experienced.

Then, one night, he received a message from an unknown number.

There was no text.

Only a photograph.

It showed the upstairs bedroom of the abandoned house.

The yellow lamp was burning.

The family photograph rested beside it.

And standing in front of the photograph was Ananya.

She was staring directly at the camera.

Behind her stood Ravi.

His face was twisted into a cruel smile.

Between them stood Chinnu.

The boy was holding a butterfly.

Arjun enlarged the photograph.

At the very edge of the frame, he noticed something else.

An old man.

Veeranna.

He was standing in the corner of the room.

His face was covered in blood.

And his finger was pressed against his lips.

Below the photograph was a single sentence.

YOU ASKED WHO WAS ALIVE.

Arjun's phone began vibrating.

Another message arrived.

NOW ASK WHO IS DEAD.

He dropped the phone.

From the darkness behind him came three knocks.

KNOCK. KNOCK. KNOCK.

A child's voice whispered:

"Uncle... I'm hungry."

Arjun turned around.

Nobody was there.

But the room had become freezing cold.

The smell of sulfur filled the air.

And on the wall, a yellow light began to flicker.

For a moment, Arjun saw four shadows.

Then five.

Then six.

One of them raised its hand.

And pointed toward him.

TO BE CONTINUED...

Ananya remains missing.

Ravi remains missing.

Veeranna has disappeared.

Nobody knows whether Chinnu's spirit is trapped in the house or whether something else is pretending to be him.
