ANIMAL KINGDOM

Book Five — Part One

A Fight with the Invisible Enemy

A story by Vijay Krishna

Start Reading ↓

The first thunder struck before midnight.

It was so powerful that birds fell from the trees, frightened deer crashed through the bushes, and the ground trembled beneath the paws of sleeping animals.

Then came the rain.

It poured from the sky like an endless waterfall. Lightning split the clouds, turning the forest white for a single terrifying moment before darkness swallowed everything again.

The trees shook violently. Their branches twisted in the wind, and enormous roots rose from the wet earth. Grass flattened beneath the storm. Rivers overflowed, and muddy water rushed between the roots.

The forest had seen storms before.

It had survived fires, droughts, invasions, and terrible wars.

But it had never experienced anything like this.

Because something was moving beneath the rain.

Something that did not leave footprints.

Something that could pass through the darkness without disturbing a single leaf.

Something that was watching every animal.

And somewhere beneath the deepest lake, an old enemy had opened his eyes.

The crocodile was back.

He rose slowly from the black water, his enormous body covered in mud and ancient scars. Around his neck hung a dark necklace that seemed to swallow the light around it. Strange markings moved across its surface, appearing and disappearing like living shadows.

The crocodile lifted his head.

Then he laughed.

It was not an ordinary laugh. It sounded like stones grinding beneath the earth, like a hundred frightened animals crying from inside a cave.

The sound travelled through the water and into the forest.

Every animal that heard it felt a terrible chill run down its spine.

The crocodile smiled.

"How beautiful," he whispered. "They still believe this forest belongs to them."

Lightning flashed above him.

His eyes glowed.

"They have forgotten who ruled this land before the lions."

He slowly disappeared beneath the water.

A moment later, the lake became perfectly still.

The storm continued.

But the crocodile was no longer there.

✦   ✦   ✦

Rudra woke when a tree crashed outside his den.

His injured leg still troubled him, and the old scars across his body ached whenever the weather changed. He rose carefully and looked into the darkness.

Veeran, his son, was sleeping nearby.

"Veeran," Rudra whispered. "Wake up."

The young wolf lifted his head.

"What happened?"

"Something is wrong."

Rudra stepped outside.

The wind struck his face. Rain soaked his fur, and thunder rolled across the hills. He looked toward the forest boundary, where the trees were bending as if something enormous were pushing through them.

But there was nothing there.

No animal.

No movement.

Only darkness.

Then a deep laugh echoed from somewhere beyond the river.

Veeran moved closer to his father.

"Was that a crocodile?"

Rudra listened carefully.

"I don't know."

Another laugh came from a different direction.

Then another.

The sounds seemed to move around them, crossing the forest without following any path.

Rudra's ears rose.

"Wake the others. Tell them to stay together."

Veeran ran toward the dens.

Rudra remained beneath the rain, staring into the darkness.

For the first time since fighting Kaalakesari and Nagu, he felt something he could not easily explain.

Not fear of a stronger enemy.

Fear of an enemy he could not find.

✦   ✦   ✦

By morning, the forest had become a place of terror.

Animals gathered near the great clearing beneath Simha's apple tree. Their fur was soaked, their eyes were wide, and many refused to leave the safety of the others.

A family of deer reported hearing footsteps behind them all night, but whenever they turned, nothing was there.

A bear said something had whispered his name from inside an empty cave.

Several birds claimed that an invisible creature had chased them through the trees without touching a single branch.

The elephants found enormous claw marks in the mud.

The marks began beside the river and ended in the middle of a patch of untouched grass.

There was no trail beyond them.

Even the snakes had disappeared from the lower tunnels.

Puli and Jagu stood near the edge of the gathering.

Puli's powerful shoulders were tense. Jagu's spotted fur was wet from the rain, and his eyes moved constantly between the trees.

"Something is hunting us," Puli growled.

Jagu lowered his head to examine the mud.

"Then why can't we find its tracks?"

"Perhaps it can fly."

"Then why are the marks in the ground?"

Puli had no answer.

A sudden scream came from the far side of the clearing.

Every animal turned.

A young rabbit stood alone beside a fallen tree. Its eyes were wide with terror.

"Something touched me!"

The rabbit backed away.

"I couldn't see it, but something touched my back!"

The grass behind it bent.

Slowly.

As if an invisible animal were walking through it.

The grass continued bending toward the clearing.

Then it stopped.

The rabbit fainted.

Nobody moved.

Rudra stepped forward, his son beside him.

"Everyone stay together. Nobody enters the forest alone."

His voice was calm, but the animals could hear the tension beneath it.

Simha's son stood near Bujji, trying to hide his fear. He had grown stronger under Rudra's guidance, but this was unlike any enemy he had ever faced.

He knew how to fight a predator.

He knew how to defend a territory.

But how could he fight something he could not even see?

Another thunderclap shook the clearing.

The young lion flinched.

For a moment, he saw his father's death again. He remembered the battles, the blood, and the terrible loneliness that had followed.

He could not bear the thought of losing everyone he loved.

He turned and hurried toward Rudra.

"Rudra!"

The old wolf looked at him.

The young lion tried to speak, but fear caught in his throat.

Rudra stepped closer.

"Stay beside me."

The lion lowered his head.

"I'm afraid."

Rudra placed a paw on his shoulder.

"So am I."

The young lion looked up in surprise.

Rudra continued.

"Courage doesn't mean you feel no fear. It means you don't let fear make you abandon those who need you."

He looked toward the dark forest.

"But we must understand what we are facing before we can fight it."

The young lion stood beside him.

For the first time that morning, he felt he was not alone.

Then a voice came from the rain.

"How touching."

Every animal froze.

The voice was deep, rough, and full of amusement.

"Simha's little son hiding behind an old wolf."

Rudra turned toward the river.

"Show yourself!"

A laugh answered him.

"Why?"

The voice seemed to come from the trees, the river, and the ground beneath their feet.

"You cannot see me."

The animals began to tremble.

Then the river rose.

A massive shape emerged from the water.

Two glowing eyes appeared first, followed by a broad, scarred head and a mouth filled with enormous teeth.

The crocodile climbed onto the muddy bank.

The dark necklace around his neck shone beneath the lightning.

He looked across the terrified animals and laughed again.

"Did you miss me?"

✦   ✦   ✦

The crocodile's name was never spoken in the old histories.

Some called him the Shadow Crocodile.

Others called him the Ancient One.

But the oldest creatures of the forest remembered a different name whispered in stories from long before Simha's family ruled the kingdom.

The crocodile looked at the young lion.

"Your father never told you the whole story, did he?"

Simha's son stepped forward despite his fear.

"What do you want?"

The crocodile's smile widened.

"What was taken from me."

Rudra moved between the crocodile and the young lion.

"You will not touch him."

The crocodile looked down at the wolf.

"Rudra. The broken Alpha. The warrior who lost almost his entire pack and still believes he can protect everyone."

Rudra's eyes hardened.

"Leave the forest."

The crocodile laughed so loudly that the animals flinched.

"Leave? This land was mine before your precious lions ever sat beneath that apple tree."

He turned toward the ancient tree.

"Simha's great ancestors took my family's kingdom. They built their homes upon our history and called themselves kings."

His eyes returned to the young lion.

"And now his son stands before me, wearing the future that should have belonged to my bloodline."

Simha's son trembled, but he did not retreat.

"If you believe this kingdom was stolen, why attack innocent animals who never knew your family?"

For the first time, the crocodile stopped laughing.

Then his expression became colder.

"Because innocent animals are the easiest to control when they are afraid."

He raised his head.

"And soon, every creature in this forest will understand what fear truly means."

Lightning struck a tree behind him.

The crocodile slipped into the river.

His tail disappeared beneath the water.

And then the entire clearing went silent.

The storm stopped.

The rain ceased.

The wind died.

For several seconds, the forest was completely still.

Then a terrible sound rose from the earth.

A howl.

Not from a wolf.

Not from any animal the forest recognized.

It came from somewhere beneath the ground.

And it was answered by a hundred whispers.

✦   ✦   ✦

That night, the animals gathered inside the ancient stone shelter near the apple tree.

Nobody slept.

Puli and Jagu guarded the entrance. Rudra stayed close to Simha's son, while Veeran watched the shadows beyond the walls.

Bujji sat in the corner, holding his knees against his chest.

He had told countless stories about Simha. He had laughed through danger and comforted the young lion through grief.

But now he had nothing to say.

He kept staring at the dark necklace he had seen around the crocodile's neck.

Something about it frightened him more than the crocodile himself.

He remembered an old story told by a travelling owl many years ago.

A necklace forged in the deepest darkness.

A relic that could bend dreams, reveal thoughts, and twist what animals believed they saw.

A necklace that could make an enemy appear where it was not.

Bujji slowly raised his head.

"Rudra."

The wolf turned.

"What is it?"

Bujji swallowed.

"I think the crocodile's necklace is the key."

Before Rudra could answer, the stone shelter shook.

A crack appeared in the wall.

Then another.

The torches went out.

Darkness swallowed the room.

Something moved behind them.

Puli spun around.

"Who's there?"

No answer came.

Jagu's claws scraped against the stone.

"I can hear breathing."

"From where?" Puli demanded.

Jagu looked into the darkness.

"Everywhere."

Then the first scream began.

✦   ✦   ✦

The invisible enemy attacked before dawn.

The animals had barely left the shelter when the forest erupted into chaos.

A deer was dragged backward into the bushes by something nobody could see. A bear charged at an empty patch of grass and was thrown violently against a tree. Birds fell from the sky as invisible forces struck them in midair.

The enemy did not attack like an ordinary predator.

It appeared to be everywhere.

One moment, the clearing was empty.

The next, trees snapped, stones flew through the air, and animals were thrown aside by unseen blows.

Rudra shouted commands.

"Stay together! Protect the young! Do not chase anything into the forest!"

But panic spread faster than his voice.

The animals could not defend themselves against an enemy that had no visible body.

Puli stood beside Jagu.

"Whatever this thing is, it can be hurt!"

Jagu bared his teeth.

"Then let it come close!"

A powerful blow struck the ground between them.

The earth split.

Both cats jumped away as a massive tree fell where they had been standing.

Puli looked at Jagu.

"Still think you can fight it?"

Jagu's eyes narrowed.

"I think I can make it regret choosing us."

They moved together into the trees, using their knowledge of the forest to track the invisible creature.

For years, they had been rivals.

Now they moved like one warrior with two bodies.

Puli listened for sounds while Jagu studied the ground. Wherever the grass bent or the mud shifted, Jagu marked the direction. Whenever branches snapped, Puli charged toward the sound.

They found a trail leading toward the river.

Then it vanished.

Jagu crouched.

"Nothing."

Puli sniffed the ground.

"Something was here."

A whisper came from behind them.

"You are very clever."

They turned.

Nothing stood there.

The voice continued.

"But clever animals die just as easily as foolish ones."

Jagu leaped toward the sound.

His claws struck empty air.

Then something invisible slammed into his chest.

Jagu crashed against a tree.

"Puli!" he gasped.

Puli rushed to him, but another unseen blow struck the ground. The two warriors were separated by a wall of falling branches.

Jagu tried to stand.

A dark shape flickered before him.

For a fraction of a second, he saw a long body covered in wet scales.

Then it disappeared.

Jagu's eyes widened.

"Crocodile!"

The creature laughed.

"You saw me."

Jagu backed away.

"Show yourself!"

The invisible force struck again.

Jagu was thrown into the mud.

Puli heard his friend's cry and charged through the fallen branches.

But by the time he reached the place, Jagu was gone.

Only his pawprints remained in the mud.

Puli stared at them.

"Jagu?"

The forest answered with silence.

Then, somewhere far away, came a sound that made his blood run cold.

The crocodile laughing.

✦   ✦   ✦

Puli searched for Jagu through the storm.

He crossed the riverbank, climbed over broken trees, and called his friend's name until his throat hurt.

He found torn branches and deep marks in the mud.

He found blood on a stone.

But he could not find Jagu.

Finally, he reached a narrow passage between two enormous rocks.

There, he saw a familiar shape lying beneath the rain.

"Jagu!"

Puli rushed forward.

Jagu was badly wounded. His spotted fur was soaked, and his breathing was weak. He tried to lift his head when Puli reached him.

"You came," Jagu whispered.

Puli lowered himself beside his friend.

"Of course I came. I'm not leaving you here."

Jagu tried to smile.

"I thought you might finally enjoy having the forest to yourself."

Puli let out a broken laugh.

"Stop talking like that. We're going home."

He tried to lift Jagu, but his friend cried out in pain.

Puli froze.

He looked at the wounds and understood that Jagu could barely move.

"Hold on," Puli whispered. "I'll find Rudra. I'll bring help."

Jagu caught his paw.

"Puli."

"Don't speak."

"Listen to me."

Puli shook his head.

"No. You listen to me. You are not dying here."

Jagu looked into his friend's eyes.

For years, they had fought over territory, challenged one another, and argued about who was the better warrior. They had once believed that only one of them could be the strongest.

But Simha had taught them that strength meant little if the forest was divided.

Since then, Puli had never imagined a future without Jagu beside him.

Jagu's voice grew weaker.

"You were always too stubborn."

Puli's eyes filled with tears.

"And you were always too proud."

Jagu gave a faint smile.

"Then I suppose we made a good pair."

A shadow moved across the rocks.

Puli turned.

The crocodile stood behind them, his dark necklace shining through the rain.

Puli rose, placing himself between the creature and Jagu.

"You want someone to fight? Fight me!"

The crocodile smiled.

"I already have."

He moved forward.

Puli attacked.

He struck with his claws, leaped toward the crocodile's head, and tried to drive him away from Jagu.

But the necklace flashed.

Puli suddenly saw dozens of crocodiles surrounding him.

He spun toward one, then another. Every image looked real. Every shadow seemed ready to attack.

The real crocodile moved between the illusions.

Puli struck empty air.

A powerful blow sent him tumbling down the rocks.

He struggled to rise, but his body refused to obey.

"Jagu!" he cried.

The crocodile approached the wounded jaguar.

Jagu tried to crawl away.

He could not.

Puli dragged himself across the ground, screaming his friend's name.

"NO! LEAVE HIM ALONE!"

The crocodile lowered his head.

Jagu looked at Puli one final time.

There was no fear in his eyes now.

Only sadness.

"Don't let them make you hate the world," he whispered.

Then the crocodile seized him.

Puli watched in horror as the creature dragged Jagu toward the river. He tried to reach them, but the invisible force struck him again, pinning him against the rocks.

He could not move.

He could only watch.

The crocodile disappeared into the dark water with Jagu.

The river closed over them.

Puli stared at the place where his friend had vanished.

For several seconds, he made no sound.

Then a terrible cry escaped his throat.

"JAGU!"

His roar echoed through the forest.

He struck the ground with his claws, again and again, until his paws bled. He called Jagu's name until his voice broke.

But the river gave him no answer.

Puli lowered his head.

The friend he had fought beside, argued with, and trusted with his life was gone.

And he had been unable to save him.

✦   ✦   ✦

When Puli returned to the clearing, the animals gathered around him.

Simha's son rushed forward.

"Where is Jagu?"

Puli could not look at him.

"He's gone."

The young lion froze.

Bujji covered his mouth.

Rudra closed his eyes.

Even the elephants lowered their heads.

Puli walked past them and lay beneath a tree, staring into the rain.

Nobody tried to comfort him.

They understood that some pain was too deep for words.

But the crocodile was not finished.

He had only begun to take the forest apart.

✦   ✦   ✦

The next attack came at sunset.

Veeran was helping the surviving wolves move the wounded animals into a safer shelter when the ground beneath them began to tremble.

Rudra heard the sound from the clearing.

"Veeran!"

His son turned toward the voice.

Then the trees behind him bent violently.

Something invisible rushed through the grass.

Veeran pushed a young wolf out of the way just as the creature struck.

He was thrown against a fallen tree.

Rudra ran toward him.

"VEERAN!"

The old wolf's injured leg screamed with pain, but he forced himself forward.

Veeran tried to rise.

"Father, get the others away!"

Rudra reached him.

"No. I'm not leaving you."

The invisible enemy struck again.

Rudra was thrown to the ground.

Veeran struggled to stand between his father and the unseen attacker.

"Run!" he shouted.

Rudra tried to rise, but his leg collapsed beneath him.

The dark necklace flashed in the distance.

For a moment, Rudra saw the crocodile standing among the trees.

Then the creature vanished.

Veeran looked toward his father.

His face softened.

"I wish we had more time."

Rudra shook his head violently.

"Stop talking. We are going home together."

Veeran tried to smile.

"You taught me that a wolf protects his pack."

He looked at the young wolves hiding behind the trees.

"I'm doing what you taught me."

The invisible force struck.

Veeran fell.

Rudra crawled toward him, crying his son's name.

He reached Veeran and pulled him close.

"No. Please, no."

Veeran's breathing grew weak.

Rudra pressed his forehead against his son's.

"You were supposed to live longer than me."

Veeran looked at him one final time.

"I was proud to be your son."

Then his eyes closed.

Rudra remained frozen.

The forest seemed to disappear around him.

He had lost Vanya.

He had lost almost his entire pack.

And now he had lost Veeran.

The old Alpha lowered his head over his son's body and let out a howl so full of pain that every animal in the clearing began to cry.

It was not the powerful howl that had once rallied warriors at Stone Gate.

It was the broken howl of a father whose heart had been torn apart.

The surviving wolves gathered around him.

Some lowered their heads. Others wept openly.

Rudra did not hear them.

He held his son and stared into the darkness, wishing the world would end before it could take anyone else from him.

But the crocodile was watching.

And he was smiling.

✦   ✦   ✦

Bujji had never felt so helpless.

He had lost Simha, the friend who had once protected him when he was small. He had watched Rudra lose his wife and his pack. He had seen Puli's heart break when Jagu disappeared.

Now Veeran was gone.

Bujji sat beneath the apple tree, trembling.

"I can't do this anymore," he whispered.

Nobody answered.

He looked at the stone seat where Simha had once listened to his stories.

"I used to tell everyone that everything would be all right."

His voice broke.

"I told the young lion that his father was watching over him. I told Rudra that the forest would survive. I told Puli that he wasn't alone."

He covered his face.

"But I can't make any of it better."

The rain dripped from the branches.

Bujji looked at the ground.

"Maybe I should have died with Simha."

Rudra slowly raised his head.

"Bujji."

The monkey did not look at him.

"I can't watch anyone else die. I can't watch another friend disappear."

Rudra's voice was weak.

"Then stay with us."

Bujji shook his head.

"What is the point of staying if everyone I love is taken away?"

Rudra looked at the broken forest.

For once, he had no easy answer.

He knew the feeling of wanting the pain to end. He knew what it was like to believe that the world had taken everything worth living for.

But he also knew what Simha would have said.

Rudra reached toward Bujji.

"You do not have to be strong every moment. You can cry. You can be angry. You can tell us that you are tired."

He paused.

"But don't decide that your life has no meaning because you have suffered."

Bujji looked up.

Rudra continued.

"You were there for Simha's son when he needed someone to tell him about his father. You gave him memories that no enemy can destroy. You are still part of this forest, Bujji. And we still need you."

Bujji began to cry again.

He leaned against Rudra.

For a long time, neither spoke.

Then the crocodile's laughter echoed from the river.

Bujji lifted his head.

The sound had changed.

It no longer sounded amused.

It sounded hungry.

✦   ✦   ✦

Chinna the fox had been watching the attacks from the edges of the forest.

He had spent much of his life choosing whichever side seemed most likely to benefit him. He had helped Nagu, misled the forest, and changed his loyalties when danger became too great.

But now there was nowhere to hide.

He had seen Jagu disappear.

He had watched Veeran fall.

He had heard Rudra's cries and Bujji's broken voice.

For the first time, Chinna understood that cleverness could not protect him from everything he had helped bring into the world.

He rushed toward the gathering.

"Everyone! Listen to me!"

Puli lifted his head, his eyes red from crying.

"What do you want?"

Chinna stopped.

"I know why this is happening."

Every animal turned toward him.

Chinna looked toward the river.

"The necklace."

Bujji rose slowly.

"What about it?"

"The crocodile's dark necklace is not just a weapon. It is an ancient relic. It can enter dreams, reveal thoughts, create false images, and hide the creature's movements. It makes the enemy see what the crocodile wants them to see."

Rudra's ears rose.

"Can it make an animal invisible?"

"Not exactly," Chinna replied. "It can hide its true position and confuse anyone trying to track it. It can make one place appear empty while the enemy attacks from another. It can turn fear into a weapon."

He swallowed.

"But there is more."

The animals waited.

Chinna's voice became quieter.

"The crocodile believes this forest was stolen from his ancestors. He wants Simha's bloodline destroyed, and he wants every animal in the kingdom to fear him so completely that nobody will ever challenge his rule again."

Simha's son stepped forward.

"Why kill Jagu and Veeran? Why make everyone suffer?"

Chinna lowered his head.

"Because the crocodile does not simply want to defeat the forest. He wants to destroy the bonds that make it strong."

He looked at Puli.

"He took Jagu because he knew you would grieve."

He looked at Rudra.

"He took Veeran because he knew you had already lost too much."

Finally, he looked at Bujji.

"And he wants all of you to believe that nothing remains worth protecting."

The clearing fell silent.

Chinna's eyes filled with tears.

"I helped dangerous creatures before. I believed that if I stayed clever enough, I would always survive. But I was wrong."

He looked at the young lion.

"I am sorry."

A low rumble rose from the river.

Chinna turned.

The crocodile emerged from the water.

His dark necklace glowed beneath the storm clouds.

"How touching," he said. "The fox has finally discovered the truth."

Chinna stepped back.

"Stay away from them!"

The crocodile laughed.

"You spent your life choosing sides, fox. Now you wish to stand with the heroes?"

Chinna's body trembled.

"Whatever I did before, I will not help you destroy them."

The crocodile's smile widened.

"Then you have finally chosen a side worth dying for."

He moved forward.

Chinna tried to run toward Rudra, but an invisible force seized him and dragged him across the mud.

Rudra rose with a roar.

"CHINNA!"

He charged toward the fox.

But his injured leg gave way.

He crashed to the ground.

The crocodile dragged Chinna toward the river.

Chinna clawed at the earth, leaving deep marks in the mud.

"RUDRA! LISTEN TO ME!"

Rudra tried to crawl toward him.

"Hold on!"

Chinna looked back at the animals.

"The necklace! You must find a way to break its power!"

His voice cracked.

"Do not let him make you turn against one another!"

The crocodile pulled him closer to the water.

Chinna looked at Simha's son.

"Protect the forest. Even if you are afraid."

Then he looked toward Rudra.

"I'm sorry."

The crocodile dragged him beneath the dark water.

Chinna disappeared.

The river closed over him.

For several seconds, the animals could not move.

Then Puli lowered his head and cried again.

Rudra lay on the ground, staring at the water.

Bujji covered his face.

The young lion stood trembling, looking at the river where another friend had vanished.

Three beloved animals were gone.

Jagu.

Veeran.

Chinna.

And the crocodile was still alive.

✦   ✦   ✦

The forest had never known such sorrow.

The animals gathered beneath the apple tree as night approached. The rain continued to fall, and thunder rolled above the mountains.

Puli sat alone, unable to stop crying for Jagu.

Rudra rested beside the body of his son, unable to accept that Veeran would never again walk beside him.

Bujji stared into the darkness, his heart broken by the loss of another friend.

Simha's son sat between them, remembering his father and fearing that he would lose everyone who remained.

The crocodile watched from the riverbank.

His dark necklace glowed.

He had taken their warriors, their friends, and their family members. He had turned the forest into a place where nobody felt safe.

Yet he did not attack again.

He wanted them to live with the fear.

He wanted every sound to make them tremble. Every shadow to make them wonder whether another loved one would disappear. Every moment of silence to remind them that the enemy could be anywhere.

The crocodile slowly lowered himself into the water.

Before disappearing, he looked toward the apple tree.

"Your father believed kindness would save this kingdom," he called to Simha's son.

The young lion lifted his head.

The crocodile's eyes gleamed.

"Let us see how much kindness remains after I have taken everything you love."

He vanished beneath the river.

The storm grew stronger.

Rudra raised his head and let out a weak, grieving howl.

No army answered him.

No hundred wolves joined his voice.

Only the wind carried his sorrow through the trees.

Puli looked toward the sky and cried for Jagu.

Bujji leaned against the old stone seat, wishing that he could return to the days when Simha was alive and the forest still felt safe.

The young lion looked at the dark river.

He was afraid.

He was heartbroken.

But he remembered Rudra's words.

Courage did not mean having no fear.

It meant refusing to abandon those who needed him.

He stood slowly.

"We cannot let him destroy what remains."

Rudra looked at him.

The old wolf's eyes were filled with tears, but beneath the grief, something remained.

Not certainty.

Not confidence.

A small, stubborn refusal to surrender.

The young lion looked at the surviving animals.

"We will mourn our friends. We will remember them. But we will not let the crocodile decide what kind of animals we become."

Bujji wiped his eyes.

Puli raised his head.

Rudra struggled to stand, leaning heavily on his injured leg.

The forest was broken.

Its strongest warriors were gone. Its families were frightened. Its future was uncertain.

But beneath the apple tree, the survivors remained together.

And somewhere beneath the black water, the crocodile waited with his dark necklace, certain that the forest would soon fall.

He had taken their peace.

He had taken their loved ones.

But the battle for the Animal Kingdom was not over.
