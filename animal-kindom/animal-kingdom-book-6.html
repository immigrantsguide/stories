An Original Animal Fantasy

THE ANIMAL KINGDOM

Book Six: Puli & Jaga

Two Warriors Fight for One Kingdom.

CREATED BY VIJAY KRISHNA

A story of an old rivalry, an unbreakable friendship, and a promise that survives even after death.

The Bloodstone Ground

Long before Simha became king, when Maharaja Surya ruled the forest, two powerful families hated each other. The tiger family lived in the eastern hills, while the jaguar family guarded the western cliffs. Their ancestors had once shared the same hunting grounds, but an old dispute had turned them into enemies.

Every year, the two families met at a rocky place called Bloodstone Ground. They called their contest the Trial of the Bloodstone. It was supposed to prove which family was stronger, but it had become a cruel game. Warriors fought until one side surrendered. Some never returned to their homes.

Tiger King Agni believed his family could never trust the jaguars. Jaguar King Jagan believed the same about the tigers. Their hatred was so deep that even the young warriors were taught to see the other family as enemies.

Far away, the crocodile king Karunathan watched their fights. He ruled the waterways and wore a magical necklace known as the River Crown. Its dark stone gave him power over hidden water channels. He could close streams, redirect underground rivers, and leave entire territories thirsty. While the tiger and jaguar families fought one another, Karunathan quietly took control of the water.

Surya Sees the Danger

One dry season, the rivers began to disappear. Small streams became muddy lines, pools turned to cracked earth, and animals travelled farther each day to find water. Maharaja Surya knew this was no ordinary drought. He followed the riverbed and discovered that the water was being diverted beneath the rocks.

Surya also learned that the tiger and jaguar families were still wasting their strength at Bloodstone Ground. He went to meet Agni and Jagan. Neither king welcomed him. Each demanded that Surya choose a side.

Agni: “The jaguars have been our enemies for generations. We will not stand beside them.”

Jagan: “And we will never trust the tigers.”

Surya: “While you argue over old wounds, another enemy is stealing the water that keeps every family alive. If you continue this fight, Karunathan will defeat you without even entering this valley.”

The two kings refused to believe him. Surya did not threaten them. Instead, he asked them to follow him to an ancient cave near the dry river. Inside were old drawings carved into the stone. The pictures showed tiger and jaguar warriors standing together against a great crocodile army. Their ancestors had once fought side by side to protect the forest.

Agni and Jagan stared at the drawings. Their own history had been forgotten. The hatred they had inherited was not the whole truth.

Surya asked them to make a new agreement: no more hunting or attacking one another, shared access to water and hunting grounds, and a promise that both families would defend the forest together. After a long silence, Agni extended his paw. Jagan accepted it.

The Dream of the Ancestors

That night, Surya slept beside the old cave. In his dream, he saw the great rulers who had protected the forest before him. At their head stood Maharaja Aravind, an ancient lion king. They showed Surya the River Crown and explained that its dark gem was connected to the deepest spring beneath the crocodile kingdom.

They warned him that Karunathan's power would not end while the gem remained connected to the underground channels. The crocodiles were not alone: giant pythons guarded the tunnels and attacked anyone who tried to reach the spring. The ancestors also told Surya that the hippos knew the waterways better than any other creatures. Their strength could break the crocodiles' lines and expose the hidden channels.

Surya awoke before sunrise. He understood the plan. He needed allies who could fight in the water, warriors who could hold the land, and a way to reach the River Crown without being trapped underground.

The Alliance of the River

Surya travelled to the great marsh and met Bhimadanta, king of the hippos. At first, Bhimadanta was cautious. The crocodiles had threatened his people for many seasons, and he did not want to lead them into another trap.

Bhimadanta: “Karunathan controls the channels. If we enter his waters, he will turn the river against us.”

Surya: “Then we will not fight on his terms. Your people know the marsh. The elephants can break the banks. The tigers and jaguars can guard the land. Together, we can reach the source of his power.”

Bhimadanta agreed. Elephants joined the alliance, and Agni and Jagan brought warriors from both families. For the first time in many generations, tiger and jaguar fighters trained together. They learned each other's signals and defended one another during practice. Their old rivalry slowly became respect.

The War of the Broken River

Karunathan learned of the alliance and sent his crocodiles and giant pythons to destroy it before it reached the kingdom. The first attack came at the narrow river crossing. Pythons dropped from the trees while crocodiles surged from the water. The forest shook with roars, trumpets, and the crash of bodies against the riverbanks.

The elephants charged into the shallows and broke the crocodiles' formation. The hippos drove forward like moving boulders, forcing the enemy away from the crossing. Tigers guarded the left bank, while jaguars climbed the rocks and struck from above. For the first time, the two families fought as one.

The victory came at a terrible cost. Elephant warrior Dantaka fell while holding the riverbank. Jaguar warrior Karvan was lost protecting the wounded. Tiger warrior Rudhira stood his ground until the others could retreat, but he did not return. Many more brave animals died that day. Their names would be remembered by every family that survived.

Surya wanted to stop and mourn, but he knew Karunathan would use the delay to move the River Crown deeper into the kingdom. The surviving warriors chose to continue. They carried their fallen friends in their hearts and moved toward the crocodile palace.

The River Crown

The crocodile kingdom lay beneath a maze of pools, stone tunnels, and underground channels. Karunathan waited inside his palace, wearing the River Crown. The dark gem glowed whenever he moved the water. Behind him, the giant pythons guarded the entrance to the deepest spring.

Surya divided the alliance. Bhimadanta and the hippos would hold the main channel. The elephants would break the outer stone barriers. Agni and Jagan would lead the tiger and jaguar warriors through a narrow passage and reach the channel that fed the necklace.

The plan nearly failed. A python struck from a hidden tunnel and blocked the passage. Agni was trapped beneath falling stone, but Jagan and two tiger warriors pulled him free. When the tunnel began to flood, Agni returned the favour and helped the jaguar fighters reach higher ground. Their friendship was no longer just an agreement. They trusted each other with their lives.

At the deepest chamber, Jagan found the stone channel that carried power to the River Crown. He smashed the channel's support with all his strength. The water surged through the chamber, and the gem's glow flickered. Surya reached Karunathan as the crocodile king turned to attack.

Karunathan struck with his powerful tail. Surya was thrown against a stone pillar, but he rose again. He saw that the necklace was no longer protected by the full force of the river. Picking up a heavy stone hammer, Surya brought it down upon the dark gem.

The River Crown shattered.

A deep sound rolled through the underground kingdom. The hidden channels stopped obeying Karunathan. Water rushed back into its natural course. The crocodile king lost the power that had made him so dangerous. He escaped through a flooded passage, while the hippos and the alliance drove the remaining crocodile army away from the spring.

The forest's water began to return. The pythons retreated into the far tunnels, and the crocodile kingdom was no longer able to control the rivers. The war was won, but Karunathan had escaped. Among the broken stones, a small fragment of the black gem disappeared into the water.

The Promise at Bloodstone

After the war, Surya brought the surviving warriors to Bloodstone Ground. The place that had once been used for a cruel contest became a memorial for the fallen. Agni and Jagan stood together and promised that their families would never again turn against each other.

They made a new tradition. Tiger and jaguar cubs would grow up learning the names of the warriors who had died protecting both families. They would be taught that courage was not measured by defeating a neighbour, but by standing beside one when danger came.

As the years passed, the tiger and jaguar families became close allies. Their descendants carried the promise forward, even when the details of the ancient war began to fade from memory.

Bujji Finds the Story

Many generations later, after the forest had suffered the terrible events of the crocodile's return, Bujji explored an old cave near the dry river. He noticed markings hidden beneath moss and dust. He cleaned the stone and discovered pictures of Surya, the hippos, the tiger king, the jaguar king, the pythons, and the broken necklace.

Bujji hurried back and gathered the survivors beneath the old apple tree. Puli listened quietly as Bujji described the ancient feud and the promise made at Bloodstone Ground. When Bujji spoke of the jaguar king standing beside the tiger king, Puli lowered his head.

He remembered his closest friend, Jagu. Their families had once been enemies, but Puli and Jagu had built a friendship stronger than that old hatred. Jagu was gone now. The crocodile had taken his life, and no story could bring him back. But the promise between their families had survived through them.

Jagu's Promise

Before the final battle that took Jagu away, he had spoken to Puli about his young son. Jagu knew that danger was growing around the forest. He feared that one day he might not be there to guide his child.

Jagu: “Puli, brother, if anything happens to me, please reach out to my son. Do not let him feel alone. Promise me you will guide him and protect him as you would protect your own family.”

Puli: “I promise, Jagu. Whatever happens, your son will always have me.”

Jagu's son now stood beside Puli as Bujji finished telling the story. The young jaguar had heard the promise many times, but hearing the history of Agni and Jagan made him understand its deeper meaning. His father and Puli had carried forward the peace their ancestors had fought to create.

Jagu's Son: “Puli, my father told me that if he ever disappeared, I should come to you. He said you would never let me stand alone.”

Puli: “Your father was my brother. I could not save him, but I gave him my word that I would protect you. From this day forward, you will never fight alone.”

Puli gently touched his forehead against the young jaguar's forehead. For a moment, he remembered Jagu standing beside him in battle. The pain did not disappear, but it gave Puli strength to stand again.

The Next Battle

Bujji showed the survivors one final drawing. It showed the black gem breaking, but a tiny fragment falling into deep water. The present-day crocodile's necklace carried the same dark mark. The ancient River Crown had been destroyed, but its power had not vanished completely. The crocodile had found the missing fragment and used it to create a new necklace.

Rudra understood the danger. The crocodile was not simply attacking for territory. He was using an old power whose history the forest had forgotten. If they wanted to defeat him, they would need to understand the waterways and find the source of the necklace's power.

Surya's old alliance had left descendants across the forest. The hippos answered the call. Elephant families sent their strongest warriors. The tiger and jaguar families stood together again. Puli would lead the tiger warriors, and Jagu's son would stand beside him with the jaguar fighters.

The young jaguar looked at Puli. He was afraid, but he did not step back.

Jagu's Son: “I want to fight beside you, just as my father did.”

Puli: “Then fight with courage, but never forget that protecting one another is more important than winning glory.”

Far away, beneath the dark water, the crocodile watched the gathering armies. His necklace pulsed with a faint black light. He remembered Karunathan's defeat and the old River Crown. He would not make the same mistake. He would use fear, confusion, and the hidden channels to divide the forest before the battle began.

But he had forgotten one thing. The tiger and jaguar families were no longer enemies. The friendship between Puli and Jagu had become a promise carried by Jagu's son. No illusion could erase it, and no darkness could make them turn against one another again.

Puli looked toward the river. He could almost hear his friend's voice in the wind.

Puli: “Jagu, I kept my promise. Your son is beside me. And together, we will protect this forest.”

The old war had ended. The old friendship remained. And a new battle was about to begin.
