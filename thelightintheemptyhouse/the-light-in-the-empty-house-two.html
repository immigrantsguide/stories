A Supernatural Horror Story

The Light in the Empty House

Part 2 — The Village That Feared the Dead

The village had stopped sleeping.

After everything that happened in the empty house, Arjun thought the worst was behind him. He was wrong.

Something had awakened.

Something that had been buried beneath the village long before the first house was built.

And now it was hungry.

The morning after the strange incident, Arjun walked towards the tea stall where he had met the old villagers during his first visit.

The stall was closed.

A black thread hung across its entrance. Three lemons, covered in red powder, lay beneath it. A small clay pot stood upside down beside the door.

Arjun knocked. Nobody answered.

He walked towards the old man's house. The old man had told him about the empty house. He had warned him not to visit it after sunset.

Arjun knocked repeatedly. Finally, the door opened. The old man stood there, trembling.

"Please go away," he whispered.

"Why? What happened?"

The old man looked behind Arjun. Then he slowly closed the door.

Arjun heard him say something from inside.

"He has brought it back."

That evening, he visited Raghava and Lakshmi.

The house looked different. Every window was covered with black cloth. A small oil lamp burned outside the entrance.

Lakshmi opened the door. She looked at Arjun and immediately stepped backwards.

"Why have you come here again?"

"I need answers. What happened after I left?"

Raghava appeared behind her.

"We told you not to come back."

"Listen, something is happening in this village. People are disappearing. Nobody is telling me anything."

Raghava grabbed his arm.

"You don't understand. The dead are walking again."

Before Arjun could ask another question, a loud metallic sound came from their backyard.

CLANG!

A steel plate was lying on the ground. But there was something written on it.

ARJUN.

Raghava shut the door in his face.

That night, the village temple bell rang thirteen times.

There was no priest inside the temple. And nobody had touched the bell.

The new deaths began three days later.

A lorry driver travelling along the highway reported seeing a young woman standing in the middle of the road. She wore a white dress. Her face was hidden by long black hair.

He stopped his lorry.

"Are you alright?" he shouted.

The woman raised her hand and pointed towards the village.

The driver opened his door. He never reached the village.

The following morning, villagers found his lorry abandoned near the banyan tree. The windshield was covered in blood.

The driver's body was discovered hanging upside down inside the cargo compartment. His eyes were open. His mouth was stuffed with black soil.

On the inside of the driver's door, someone had scratched a sentence:

SHE WAS NOT ALONE.

Two nights later, an auto driver stopped for another woman on the same road.

He called his wife before leaving.

"Someone needs help. I'll drop her and come."

His wife heard a strange sound. A woman's laughter. Then the phone went silent.

The next morning, his auto was found near the old bridge. The driver's body was inside.

His hands were tied together, and his head was turned completely towards the back seat. There was nobody sitting there.

After that, no driver stopped on that road. Soon, even the highway transport companies stopped sending vehicles through that route.

The road became abandoned.

THE ROAD OF THE LAST RIDE.

Arjun's phone had stopped receiving signals. He had tried calling several times, but every call ended with the same message.

NO NETWORK.

He had no idea that someone was desperately trying to reach him.

His girlfriend, Meera, had been calling him for two days. She had decided to visit the village herself.

She took a bus to the nearest town and hired a vehicle to reach the village. The driver refused to go beyond the old bridge.

"I can drop you here. I won't go further."

"Why?"

He looked at her.

"Because nobody who travels that road after dark comes back."

Meera laughed nervously. She paid him and continued on foot.

Her phone had only one bar of signal. She called Arjun. The call didn't connect. She tried again. Nothing.

Then she saw an auto approaching from the opposite direction. The driver stopped.

"Madam, where are you going?"

"To the village."

The driver stared at her.

"Nobody goes there."

"I need to meet someone."

The driver hesitated, then opened the door. Meera climbed inside.

After a few minutes, she noticed that the driver was no longer looking at the road. He was staring at the rear-view mirror.

"What happened?"

He whispered:

"Madam... who is sitting behind you?"

Meera turned around. The back seat was empty.

When she looked forward again, the driver was screaming. Something black was crawling across the roof of the auto.

It had long fingers. Its head was bent backwards.

The auto stopped. The engine died.

Meera tried opening the door. It wouldn't move.

A voice came from outside.

"Arjun..."

She froze. It sounded exactly like Arjun.

"Meera... open the door."

She reached for the handle. The driver grabbed her wrist.

"Don't listen! That's not him!"

The windows began to crack. A black hand pushed through the glass.

Then everything went dark.

The next morning, villagers found the auto near the abandoned road. The driver was dead. Meera was gone.

Her phone was lying on the passenger seat. Its screen was still lit.

There was one unfinished message:

Arjun, I am near your village. Someone is following me.

Nobody ever found her body. Nobody knew what had happened to her.

And Arjun never received the message.

The police arrived after the third driver was found dead. But they did not come alone.

They brought armed officers, medical personnel and a special investigation team. Their leader was Inspector Pratap.

He had investigated murders, missing persons and criminal gangs for more than twenty years. He was not a man who believed in ghosts.

But after entering the village, even he began to feel uncomfortable.

Every house had the same strange arrangement. A black thread was tied across every entrance. Iron nails were fixed above the doors. Small pots containing ash and red powder were placed outside. Some houses had animal skulls hanging from their roofs.

At sunset, every villager performed the same ritual.

They placed a bowl of salt outside their homes. They lit five oil lamps facing different directions. Then they closed every window.

At exactly midnight, they rang a small bell three times.

Nobody spoke. Nobody opened their doors.

Inspector Pratap asked an elderly woman about the ritual.

"We are not protecting ourselves from the dead, sir."

"Then from whom?"

She looked towards the forest.

"From the thing that wears the dead."

Pratap laughed. But the woman did not.

"It comes without footsteps. It can enter a locked house. It can call your name using the voice of someone you love."

"How do you stop it?"

"You don't stop it. You only make it forget your house for one night."

"Who taught you this?"

She pointed towards the old temple.

"The people who lived here before us."

The first victim inside the village was the old man who had spoken to Arjun.

His body was discovered sitting in front of his house. His head was lowered. His hands were folded as if he were praying.

But when the police examined him, they found that his shadow was pointing in the opposite direction from the sunlight. His eyes were completely black.

On his wall, written in blood, were four words:

HE BROUGHT THE LIGHT BACK.

Inspector Pratap immediately asked about Arjun.

"Who is this man?"

Nobody answered.

He questioned Raghava. Then Lakshmi. Then the tea stall owner.

Every person gave the same answer.

"Arjun came here. After that, everything started."

The tea stall owner was found dead the following night. The temple caretaker disappeared. A farmer whom Arjun had questioned during his first visit was discovered dead inside his locked cattle shed.

His animals were alive. But all of them were facing the same corner.

The corner was completely dark.

One by one, the people Arjun had met during his previous visit were dying. And every time, the police found the same message.

ARJUN WAS HERE.

The villagers began to whisper. Some believed Arjun had brought a curse. Others believed he was not human.

One evening, a little boy saw Arjun walking near the temple. He ran inside his house screaming.

His mother asked what had happened.

The boy replied:

"Amma, the man walking outside has two shadows."

Inspector Pratap finally summoned Arjun to the police station.

"Where were you on the nights of these murders?"

"At my house."

"Who can confirm that?"

"Nobody."

"Convenient."

Arjun slammed his hand on the table.

"What are you trying to say?"

Pratap opened a file. Inside were photographs of the dead villagers.

"You visited every one of these people. After meeting you, they died."

"Do you think I killed them?"

"I think you know something."

"I came here to investigate a disappearance!"

"Which disappearance?"

Arjun remained silent.

Pratap leaned forward.

"Who are you really?"

Arjun stood up.

"I'm leaving this village."

Two officers blocked the door.

Pratap slowly got up.

"You are not leaving."

"Move."

"You are a suspect in multiple murders. Until this investigation is over, you stay here."

Arjun pushed one officer aside. The second officer grabbed his shoulder. Arjun turned and struck him.

Within seconds, the police station became a battlefield. Two officers tried to restrain him. Arjun fought back, knocking one into a wooden bench.

Pratap pulled out his gun.

"STOP!"

Arjun froze.

"Put your hands up!"

"You're protecting something. You don't want me to leave because you know what's happening here."

Pratap's expression changed. For a moment, he looked genuinely frightened.

"You're right. I don't want you to leave."

"Why?"

"Because the moment you cross the village boundary, something follows you."

Arjun stared at him.

Pratap lowered his gun.

"We have already tried sending people out. They never reached the next town."

"Then let me try."

"No."

"You're keeping me prisoner!"

Pratap stepped closer.

"Maybe. But you're staying alive as long as you're inside this village."

That same night, another vehicle entered the village.

It was a black SUV. A young man stepped out. His name was Vikram.

He was a former criminal investigation officer who had left the police department after a mysterious case in another district.

He had received a letter from a retired officer about the murders. He carried a camera, a notebook and an old photograph of the village.

Unlike the police, Vikram did not believe Arjun was responsible for the deaths. But he wanted to know why every victim had been connected to him.

Vikram entered the police station.

"Where is Arjun?"

Inspector Pratap looked up.

"Who are you?"

"Someone who wants to find out what is killing your people."

Pratap stared at him.

"Then you have come to the wrong place."

Vikram smiled.

"Maybe. But I noticed something on my way here."

"What?"

"Every house has five lamps outside. Every house has black threads. Every road has iron nails buried in the ground."

He placed a photograph on the table.

"But the empty house has none of them."

Pratap's face changed.

Vikram continued:

"Why is that house different?"

Nobody answered.

Then he opened another photograph. It showed the empty house. A figure was standing in the upstairs window. Behind the glass.

Its face looked exactly like Vikram.

He slowly put the photograph down.

"I took this picture before entering the village."

Vikram and Arjun met that night. Arjun told him everything: the empty house, the strange lights, the disappearances, the deaths, and the people who had warned him to leave.

Vikram listened without interrupting.

Finally, he asked:

"Did you ever enter the room at the end of the upstairs corridor?"

Arjun shook his head.

"No."

"Then we have to go back."

Arjun looked at him.

"Are you insane?"

"Maybe. But something in that house knows your name."

Before they could leave, the temple bell rang.

Once. Twice. Three times.

Then it kept ringing.

Nobody was pulling it.

The villagers began screaming. Doors slammed shut. Oil lamps went out one by one.

A woman ran across the street carrying a child. Something was following her.

It moved on all fours. Its arms were too long. Its body was covered in wet black hair.

But its face... its face looked like a human face stretched over a skull.

It stopped beneath a streetlight. Then it stood upright.

It was taller than any man. Its neck slowly twisted.

It looked directly at Arjun.

And smiled.

The woman disappeared into her house. A moment later, her screams stopped.

Vikram pulled Arjun inside the police station. Inspector Pratap locked every door.

"Nobody goes outside!" he shouted.

The officers began loading their weapons.

A loud bang came from the roof. Then another.

Something was walking above them. Slowly. Dragging its feet.

One officer looked through the window.

"Sir..."

"What?"

"There's someone standing outside."

Pratap approached the window.

A little girl stood in the rain. She was holding a doll. She looked directly at him.

"Uncle..."

Pratap stepped backwards.

The girl smiled.

"Why did you leave me?"

Pratap's gun fell from his hand.

Vikram noticed his reaction.

"Who is she?"

Pratap didn't answer.

The little girl lifted her head. Her face split into a horrible smile.

The lights went out.

Gunshots exploded in the darkness.

When the lights returned, one officer was missing. His rifle was lying on the floor.

The ceiling was covered in blood.

And something had written on the station wall:

FIVE LAMPS CANNOT STOP ME ANYMORE.

At sunrise, Vikram discovered an old book hidden beneath the temple floor.

It contained drawings of a creature. The villagers had given it a name.

THE NISHACHARA — THE NIGHT HUNTER.

According to the book, the creature had existed long before the village. It was neither a ghost nor a demon in the ordinary sense.

It was something that copied the appearance and voices of the dead. It hunted people who travelled alone after sunset.

It could imitate a loved one's voice. It could appear as a child, an old man or even someone who had died years earlier.

The five lamps and black threads were not meant to destroy it. They were meant to confuse it.

But the final page contained a warning.

WHEN THE LIGHT RETURNS TO THE EMPTY HOUSE, THE HUNTER WILL NO LONGER NEED THE DARKNESS.

Vikram read it twice. Then he looked at Arjun.

"What light?"

Arjun remembered the upstairs window. The light that appeared every night. The light that had disappeared when he entered the house.

He suddenly understood.

The empty house wasn't attracting the creature. It was keeping it trapped.

And he had opened something.

That evening, the police received an order to seal the village.

No vehicles were permitted to enter or leave. The district administration announced that the area was unsafe because of an unidentified criminal group.

But nobody believed the explanation.

A police team sent to investigate the forest refused to return. A government vehicle was discovered abandoned near the boundary.

Inside it were the officers' uniforms. Their weapons were missing. And their shadows were still visible on the seats.

Even the senior officers stopped answering calls about the village.

Inspector Pratap received one final message from his superior:

DO NOT ENTER THE EMPTY HOUSE. DO NOT ALLOW ARJUN TO LEAVE.

He looked at Arjun. Then at Vikram.

"We have lost control of this place."

At midnight, Arjun and Vikram entered the empty house.

The front door was already open.

Inside, every photograph on the wall had changed. The people in the photographs were no longer smiling. They were staring at Arjun.

The staircase creaked. A child's laughter came from upstairs.

Arjun climbed the stairs. Vikram followed him.

At the end of the corridor stood the mysterious room. The door was open.

Inside, there was a large mirror.

Arjun approached it.

His reflection was standing perfectly still. But Arjun himself was breathing heavily.

He raised his right hand. The reflection didn't move.

Instead, it slowly raised its left hand.

Then it smiled.

A voice came from behind them.

"Arjun..."

He turned.

Meera was standing at the entrance. Her white dress was covered in blood.

"Why didn't you come for me?"

Arjun couldn't move.

"Meera?"

She stepped forward.

"Look at me."

Vikram grabbed Arjun.

"Don't!"

Meera began crying.

"You left me alone."

Arjun struggled.

"Let me go!"

Vikram shouted:

"THAT IS NOT YOUR GIRLFRIEND!"

The figure's head dropped. Its neck stretched. Its jaw opened far wider than a human mouth.

Then every photograph in the room began screaming.

The mirror shattered.

Something enormous crawled out of it.

Its body was covered in human faces. Some were crying. Some were laughing. Some were begging for help.

And one of them was the old man. Another was the tea stall owner. Another was the temple caretaker.

The creature spoke in dozens of voices.

"YOU BROUGHT THE LIGHT."

Arjun fell backwards.

Vikram fired his weapon. The bullets passed through its body.

The creature lunged. The entire house shook.

A burning light appeared behind it.

For one second, Arjun saw something standing inside the light. A human-shaped figure. But it had no face.

Then the creature vanished.

The house became silent.

The following morning, Inspector Pratap found Arjun sitting outside the empty house. Vikram was nowhere to be seen.

"Where is he?" Pratap asked.

Arjun looked at the upstairs window.

"Inside."

Pratap entered the house with four armed officers. They searched every room.

There was no sign of Vikram. Only his camera remained on the floor.

Pratap picked it up. The last photograph showed Arjun standing outside the house.

But behind him were dozens of people. The dead villagers. Meera. The missing people.

And something standing much taller than the others.

Pratap dropped the camera. He looked at Arjun.

"Who are you?"

Arjun didn't answer.

Then the police station radio came alive.

A voice spoke through the static. It was Vikram.

"Inspector... don't trust Arjun."

Pratap grabbed the radio.

"Where are you?"

The voice continued.

"He's not the one who opened the door."

A pause.

"He's the reason it opened."

The radio went dead.

That night, the villagers performed their final ritual.

Every family gathered outside their homes. Five lamps were lit. Black threads were tied across every entrance.

The temple bell rang three times.

Then the entire village went silent.

At exactly 12:00 AM, every lamp went out. Every single one.

Except the light in the empty house.

It glowed brighter than ever.

The villagers fell to their knees. Some began crying. Others started chanting an old prayer.

From the forest came the sound of hundreds of footsteps.

The Night Hunter had returned.

But this time, it was not alone.

Figures emerged from the trees. The dead villagers. The missing people. The drivers. The officers.

They all walked towards the village.

And behind them stood a giant shadow.

Its head rose above the trees. Its hands dragged along the ground.

The villagers screamed. Doors broke. Lamps shattered. Black threads snapped.

The village was no longer protected.

At dawn, the police found the village empty.

No people. No bodies. No animals. Not even a single bird.

Every house was open. The temple was covered in blood. The old well was filled with black water.

And the empty house stood untouched.

Inspector Pratap entered it alone.

He found a note on the floor. It was written in Arjun's handwriting.

THEY WERE NEVER TRYING TO KEEP THE CREATURE OUT.

THEY WERE KEEPING SOMETHING ELSE INSIDE.

Pratap turned towards the mirror. His reflection was missing.

Behind him, a voice whispered:

"Inspector..."

He slowly turned.

Arjun was standing at the top of the staircase.

But his feet were not touching the floor.

Pratap raised his gun.

"Arjun?"

Arjun smiled.

Then his face began changing. First, it became the old man's face. Then the tea stall owner's. Then Meera's.

Finally, it became a face Pratap had seen in his childhood.

His dead mother's face.

Pratap dropped his weapon.

The figure whispered:

"Now you can stay with us."

The door slammed shut.

Three months later, the government officially declared the village uninhabitable.

The road was closed permanently. No transport company accepted journeys through that area.

The police station was abandoned. The investigation was marked as closed due to insufficient evidence.

But one man refused to accept the official report.

Vikram.

He had survived. He had escaped through an underground passage beneath the temple.

He was now living in another town, studying the photographs and recordings he had recovered.

One night, he received an envelope.

There was no sender's address.

Inside was a photograph.

It showed the empty house. The upstairs window was glowing.

And standing in front of it was Arjun.

Vikram stared at the photograph.

His phone rang.

Unknown number.

He answered.

At first, there was only silence.

Then a familiar voice whispered:

"Vikram..."

It was Arjun.

"Did you really think you escaped?"

The call ended.

Vikram slowly looked towards his bedroom door.

Five black threads were hanging across it.

All five had snapped.

And from the darkness outside his room came the sound of a little girl laughing.
