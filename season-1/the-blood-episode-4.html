Season One · Episode Four

The Call
of the Blood

When a father marches into darkness, even the dead must choose a side.

The tunnel had gone silent. Not peaceful—silent. The kind of silence that comes after a terrible thing has happened and the world is afraid to make another sound.

Vijay lay motionless on the cold stone floor. Mahakaali's spell had drained the strength from his body. The white-oak stick still rested near him, its tip stained with her blood. Around him, the marks of the battle were everywhere: broken chains, shattered stone, and the bodies of soldiers who had been unable to stop him.

Mahakaali stood at the tunnel entrance, breathing heavily. The spell had worked, but it had cost her more than she expected. She looked at Vijay and whispered, “You are not dead. You are not awake. And while you remain between those worlds, something older than all three kingdoms may find you.”

Far below the tunnel, a black crack opened in the rock. A cold wind rose from it. The flames in the wall torches bent toward the darkness, one after another, until every flame went out.

Then a voice came from the crack.

“The blood has been called.”

✦ ✦ ✦

In the royal fortress, Krishna stood before a huge map of the three kingdoms. Messengers had brought him the news: Vijay had been taken, the three kingdoms had joined against him, and Mahakaali had used forbidden blood magic to put him down.

Krishna did not shout. He did not throw anything. His silence frightened the commanders more than anger ever could.

“They have mistaken my patience for weakness,” he said. “They took my son into their territory. They used their armies, their monsters, and their queen's magic. Now they will answer to me.”

He turned to his commander. “Sound the war horns. Bring every loyal battalion. Send the cavalry through the eastern pass and the shadow guard through the forest road. No soldier is to burn a village or harm an innocent. But anyone standing between us and Vijay must understand what they have chosen.”

The commander struck his fist against his chest. “And if the three kingdoms refuse to surrender him?”

Krishna looked toward the dark horizon. “Then bring my son home through the battlefield.”

Across the fortress, war drums began to thunder. Armour was tightened. Blades were lifted from their racks. Thousands of soldiers moved beneath the banners of Krishna's house. At the front of the army, the royal standard rose into the night wind.

✦ ✦ ✦

The three kingdoms were already wounded. Their strongest warriors had fallen in the fight against Vijay. Their vampire and werewolf leaders were badly injured, and the remaining soldiers were exhausted. Yet Mahakaali ordered every surviving unit to the borders.

“Krishna is coming,” one captain warned. “His army is larger than the forces we have left.”

Mahakaali's eyes hardened. “Then we do not meet him as three frightened kingdoms. We stand together. If we break tonight, none of us will survive what follows.”

The Witch Queen's daughter had been watching from the shadows. She had heard every word. She had also felt something no one else could feel: a faint pulse beneath her palm, as if another heartbeat were beating inside the stone walls.

She closed her eyes. For one brief moment, she saw Vijay lying in the tunnel. A thin red thread stretched from his chest into the darkness below. At the other end of that thread, something enormous moved.

She stumbled backward.

“What did you see?” Mahakaali demanded.

“He is not alone down there,” the princess whispered. “Something has reached him. And I think it knows my name.”

Mahakaali seized her daughter's wrist. “You must never answer it. Whatever speaks from beneath the earth, do not answer.”

✦ ✦ ✦

At the border, Krishna's army met the surviving forces of the three kingdoms beneath a sky split by lightning. The first arrow flew before either side could speak. Then the battlefield erupted.

Krishna's cavalry crashed into the outer line. Shields broke. Horses reared. The soldiers of the three kingdoms fought desperately, using every narrow path and ruined watchtower to slow the advance. From the forest, werewolf fighters launched a sudden attack against the royal flank. Krishna's shadow guard answered, surrounding them between the trees.

A vampire captain leapt from a ruined tower and landed among the royal soldiers. His blade cut through the first line before Krishna's commander struck him down. More fighters poured from the darkness. The ground shook beneath the clash of steel, claws, and shields.

“Hold the line!” Krishna's commander roared. “Do not let them reach the king!”

But Krishna had already ridden forward. He dismounted in the middle of the fighting and lifted his sword. The soldiers around him saw their king standing in the storm, and the royal line surged ahead.

On the opposite side, Mahakaali watched the battle from the ridge. She knew the three kingdoms could not win a long war in their present condition. She also knew that if Krishna reached the fortress, he would tear apart every wall until he found his son.

She raised her hand. Dark symbols appeared across the clouds. The fallen soldiers on the battlefield began to twitch. One by one, their fingers moved. Their eyes opened—not with life, but with a red, empty glow.

“No,” the Witch Queen's daughter cried. “Mother, stop! You are calling them back without their souls!”

Mahakaali did not lower her hand. “If the living cannot defend these kingdoms, the dead will.”

The bodies rose.

Krishna's soldiers froze as the dead advanced through the rain. Some wore the armour of his own army. Others wore the colours of the three kingdoms. They moved with broken limbs and empty faces, and they did not stop when blades struck them.

Krishna stared at the army of the dead. “So this is what you have chosen, Mahakaali.”

He turned to his commander. “Protect the wounded. Keep the formation. Whatever happens, we do not become like them.”

✦ ✦ ✦

Deep beneath the battlefield, Vijay's fingers moved.

He was no longer in the tunnel. He stood in a vast black plain beneath a sky with no stars. A river of dark red light ran across the land. On the far bank stood a figure covered in a cloak that seemed to swallow the darkness around it.

“Who are you?” Vijay asked.

The figure did not answer. It raised one hand, and the red river stopped flowing.

“Your father's army is bleeding for you,” the voice said. “The kingdoms are raising the dead. The Witch Queen's daughter can hear you. And still you sleep.”

Vijay tried to move, but black chains tightened around his arms.

“Wake up,” the figure whispered. “Or everyone who comes to save you will die.”

Vijay pulled against the chains. A deep red light began to burn beneath his skin. The figure stepped back for the first time.

“There it is,” it said. “The blood remembers.”

In the tunnel, Vijay's chest rose sharply. The stone beneath him cracked. The white-oak stick split down the middle, and Mahakaali's spell marks began to fade.

The Witch Queen's daughter gasped and clutched her chest. She saw the black plain, the red river, and the chained figure. Then she heard Vijay's voice—not in her ears, but inside her mind.

“Don't let it out.”

She fell to her knees.

✦ ✦ ✦

On the battlefield, the dead soldiers suddenly stopped. Every empty face turned toward the fortress. Even Mahakaali's raised hand began to shake.

From beneath the earth came a sound like a giant heartbeat.

Once.

Twice.

On the third beat, the ground split between the two armies. A column of black smoke rose into the storm. The dead soldiers dropped to their knees. The living fell silent. For a moment, even the thunder seemed to disappear.

Krishna stepped toward the crack in the earth. “Vijay?”

Mahakaali looked at the opening, horror spreading across her face. She had not summoned this power. She could not control it.

The Witch Queen's daughter stood behind her, tears running down her face. “Mother,” she whispered, “the thing beneath the earth is awake.”

A hand emerged from the smoke. It was not Vijay's. Its fingers were too long, its skin black as burnt stone, and a red mark pulsed in its palm—the same mark that had appeared on Vijay's chest.

Then the voice returned, louder than before.

“Give me the boy… or I will take every kingdom.”

Krishna raised his sword. Mahakaali lowered her hand. And far below them, Vijay opened his eyes.

The war had begun for Vijay.
But something else had come to claim his blood.
