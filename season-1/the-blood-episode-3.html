THE BLOOD

The Last Spell

The Blood · Season 1, Episode 3 · Created by Vijay Krishna

The sky above Rudhiravan had never looked so terrifying.

Black clouds swallowed the moon. Lightning tore through the mountains, illuminating three ancient banners flying above the ruined fortress where the leaders of the supernatural world had gathered.

The Vampire Kingdom. The Werewolf Kingdom. The Witch Kingdom.

For the first time in twenty years, their rulers had agreed to meet in the same place. Not to celebrate peace. Not to sign a treaty. But to decide how to destroy one young man.

Vijay.

The boy they had tried to kill before he could walk. The boy who had returned twenty years later and humiliated the Vampire King inside his own palace. The boy whose existence threatened everything they had built.

Inside the fortress, hundreds of warriors stood in formation. Vampire assassins occupied the balconies. Werewolf soldiers guarded the entrances. Witches had carved protective symbols into every pillar and laid enchanted chains beneath the stone floor.

At the center of the chamber stood three enormous thrones. King Rudraveer, ruler of the vampires, sat upon the black throne. His face remained cold, but the bruises left by Vijay's earlier attack had not completely disappeared from his pride. Lord Bhairava, ruler of the werewolves, stood beside his throne, his enormous hands clenched into fists. And between them stood Queen Mahakaali.

Her black-and-silver robes moved gently despite the absence of wind. Ancient symbols glowed across her arms. A white oak staff rested in her right hand.

She had spent the entire night studying forbidden spells. She had examined ancient scrolls, consulted spirits older than the three kingdoms, and searched the deepest chambers of the Moonspire Temple. Yet every answer led to the same conclusion.

Vijay's blood could not be classified. He possessed vampire blood. He possessed werewolf blood. He carried something that resembled witch magic. But beneath all three existed another force—something that had disappeared with the fourth kingdom, something even Mahakaali feared to name.

“How many soldiers will it take?” Bhairava demanded.

Nobody answered. He turned toward Rudraveer.

“Your royal guard couldn't stop him. Your strongest mages couldn't touch him. How many more warriors must die before we admit that ordinary weapons are useless?”

“He entered my palace alone,” Rudraveer replied. “He could have killed me, but he chose to leave me alive.”

“Perhaps he wants us to gather everyone in one place,” Bhairava said. “Perhaps this meeting is exactly what he wanted.”

Mahakaali tightened her grip on the oak staff.

“Then we must ensure that he leaves this fortress alive.”

Both rulers stared at her.

“Alive?” Rudraveer asked.

The Queen's eyes flashed white.

“For now.”

A low rumble passed beneath the floor. Every candle in the chamber flickered. One witch dropped her scroll. Far below the fortress, something had moved through the ancient tunnels.

Mahakaali closed her eyes. She felt it—a presence crossing the outer boundary, a heartbeat that did not belong to any living creature.

“You called me.”

The voice entered her mind. Her eyes snapped open.

“No,” she whispered.

Bhairava stepped toward her.

“What happened?”

“He is here.”

A distant bell rang once. Twice. Then the sound stopped. The fortress fell silent. The meeting had begun.

The appointed hour passed. Ten minutes. Twenty minutes. Thirty minutes. The three rulers waited. Vampire commanders exchanged nervous glances. Werewolf warriors sniffed the air, searching for a scent they could not identify. The witches repeatedly checked the protective runes surrounding the chamber.

“Does he think we are his servants?” Bhairava finally snapped.

“He wants us frightened,” Rudraveer said coldly. “He wants us to believe that he controls this meeting before he even enters it.”

Mahakaali said nothing. She was listening—not to the warriors, not to the storm, but to the silence beyond the doors.

Then—

BOOM!

The fortress gates shook. Dust fell from the ceiling. Every warrior turned toward the entrance.

BOOM!

A second impact echoed through the corridor. A vampire captain drew his sword.

“Positions!”

Hundreds of weapons rose simultaneously.

The third impact did not come. Instead, a scream tore through the hallway. Then another. A wounded werewolf staggered into the chamber. Blood covered his armour. His eyes were wide with terror.

“Lord Bhairava…”

He collapsed. Bhairava rushed forward.

“Who did this?”

The warrior lifted a trembling hand toward the corridor. Then his body went still.

A sound emerged from the darkness. Footsteps. Slow. Unhurried. One step. Then another. The torches along the corridor began extinguishing themselves.

A vampire assassin appeared at the far end of the hallway, running toward the chamber. Behind him, a young man walked through the smoke. Black coat. Dark hair. Blood-red eyes. A faint golden flame burning deep within his pupils.

Vijay.

The assassin screamed and attacked. Vijay caught him by the throat, lifted him from the ground, and hurled him against the stone wall with terrifying force. The impact shook the corridor.

The remaining guards charged together. Vijay did not slow down. He moved through them like a shadow released from a nightmare. Steel rang, armour split, and bodies crashed against the walls. Blood streaked the ancient stones. The guards' screams echoed down the corridor, swallowed one by one by the storm outside.

He did not fight like someone trying to reach a destination. He fought like someone who had already decided that nothing standing between him and his destination deserved to remain standing.

At the chamber entrance, two enormous werewolf guards crossed their axes.

“STOP!”

Vijay stopped. For a moment, he looked almost amused.

The first guard roared and swung his weapon. Vijay caught the axe. The second guard attacked from behind. Vijay turned, struck him aside, and smashed the first guard's weapon against the stone floor. Both warriors fell.

Vijay stepped over them. The enormous doors stood before him. He placed one hand against the metal.

Inside the chamber, Mahakaali raised her staff.

“Nobody moves until I give the order.”

Rudraveer summoned shadows around his fingers. Bhairava's claws emerged.

The doors began opening. Slowly. The hinges screamed. Cold air swept through the chamber, extinguishing half the candles.

And Vijay entered.

Blood stained his sleeves. Smoke curled behind him. His eyes moved across the hundreds of armed warriors, the three thrones, and finally the Witch Queen standing at the center.

“Sorry I'm late.”

Nobody laughed. Vijay looked at the fallen guards outside.

“Your hospitality is terrible.”

He walked forward. Every warrior moved aside—not because their rulers had commanded them to, but because none of them wanted to discover what happened if they stood in his way.

Vijay stopped beneath the three royal banners. He looked at Rudraveer, then Bhairava, and finally Mahakaali.

“Well? You wanted to see me.”

He spread his arms.

“Now look at me.”

Bhairava descended from the platform. His enormous frame seemed to fill the chamber.

“You have entered a royal meeting after murdering our guards.”

“Your guards attacked me,” Vijay replied.

“They were protecting the fortress!”

“And I was protecting my patience.”

A murmur passed through the warriors. Bhairava's eyes turned amber.

“This is not your kingdom, boy.”

“Then why did your boundary open for me?”

Bhairava's expression hardened. Rudraveer rose from his throne.

“You will show respect to the rulers of Rudhiravan.”

“Respect?” Vijay took one step closer. “You ordered soldiers to burn my home. You tried to erase my existence. You concealed the truth about my family. And now you want respect?”

His voice grew colder.

“Tell me, King Rudraveer. Which part of that history should I respect?”

Rudraveer summoned a blade of black energy.

“You are standing here because I allow you to stand here.”

Vijay laughed—a short, bitter laugh.

“That is the problem with you, Uncle. You still think you are the most powerful person in every room.”

His golden pupils ignited.

“That mistake is going to hurt you.”

Bhairava stepped between them.

“Enough! You will leave Rudhiravan immediately. You will never return to our borders, never approach our people, and never threaten our kingdoms again.”

Vijay looked at him. For a brief moment, his expression softened.

“Do you remember my father?”

Bhairava froze.

“Do you remember what you promised him?”

The werewolf lord's jaw tightened.

“Your father made choices that endangered us all.”

Vijay's face changed.

“And there it is. The same excuse. The same lies. The same cowards hiding behind the word kingdom.”

Bhairava's claws extended.

“You will leave!”

Vijay looked at the three rulers, then at the army, then back at Mahakaali.

“No. I did not come here to ask permission to exist.”

He stepped into the center of the chamber.

“I came for answers. Where is my mother?”

Nobody answered.

“Where is my father?”

Still nothing. He looked at Mahakaali.

“You know something.”

“Vijay, you do not understand what you are,” the Queen said.

“Then explain it.”

“I cannot.”

“Cannot? Or will not?”

Mahakaali lifted her staff.

“You are carrying a power that could destroy every living soul in this land.”

Vijay looked at his hands.

“Perhaps you should have thought of that before you tried to burn me alive.”

“Your anger is making you dangerous.”

“No. Your fear is making me angry.”

A violent pulse of energy burst from his body. Every candle went out. The floor cracked beneath his boots. Somewhere deep within the fortress, an ancient seal answered his presence.

Mahakaali struck the floor with her white oak staff. A circle of white fire erupted beneath Vijay. Ancient runes rose into the air, surrounding him in a cage of light.

“By the first flame, the first root, and the first name, I bind the blood that defies its origin!”

The circle contracted. Vijay's body jerked. His golden eyes dimmed. He dropped to one knee.

The warriors stared in disbelief. Rudraveer smiled. Bhairava raised his hand.

“Now! Before he recovers!”

The Queen's voice thundered through the chamber.

“WAIT!”

Black chains emerged from the floor and wrapped around Vijay's arms, legs, and chest. His head lowered. His breathing became shallow. The magic was working. Mahakaali felt his power collapsing beneath her spell.

“Is he finished?” Rudraveer asked.

“I am holding him,” the Queen replied.

The Queen's fingers tightened around her staff. The chains trembled. Vijay laughed—quietly, almost gently.

“That. You actually believe you have won.”

His eyes flashed gold. The chains cracked. Mahakaali immediately tightened the spell. The runes blazed brighter. Vijay's body collapsed forward, blood trickling from the corner of his mouth.

“Silence!”

The chains shattered. Vijay stood. The white fire vanished. The entire chamber shook. Mahakaali stumbled backward.

“Your magic is beautiful, Queen,” Vijay said. “But something is wrong with it.”

He looked toward the soldiers.

“Your turn is over. Now it's mine.”

The first hundred warriors attacked together. Vampire assassins descended from the balconies. Werewolf soldiers charged from both sides. Witches unleashed bolts of blue fire, silver lightning, and binding spells. For one terrible moment, Vijay disappeared beneath a storm of weapons and magic.

The chamber erupted. Steel struck stone. Spells exploded against the pillars. The floor cracked beneath the force of hundreds of supernatural warriors. A wave of fire swept across the eastern aisle, turning the banners to ash. Warriors were hurled into columns; shattered shields and broken weapons skidded through blood-slick marble. The wounded cried out beneath the roar of the battle, and the dead lay where they fell as the fighting surged around them.

“Do not let him escape!” Rudraveer shouted.

“Bring him down!” Bhairava roared.

Vijay emerged through the smoke. His coat was torn. Blood covered his chest. A deep wound crossed his shoulder. He looked furious—not with the controlled anger of a warrior, nor the cold hatred of a king, but with something older, something that had spent twenty years locked inside a child's heart.

A vampire commander attacked from the left. Vijay caught his sword and drove him into the ground. A werewolf lunged at Vijay's back. Vijay spun, struck him aside, and sent him crashing through a wooden barricade. Three witches began chanting. Their spell formed a cage of silver light around Vijay's body.

He staggered. His knees struck the floor. For several seconds, he could not move. Mahakaali immediately recognized the spell.

“Hold it! Do not break the circle!”

The witches joined their power. Vijay's arms trembled. His head lowered. His breathing became ragged. The spell was working again.

“Finish him!” Rudraveer commanded.

The vampire king raised a spear of black energy. But before he could release it, Vijay looked up. His eyes had become entirely golden.

“What did I tell you?”

The silver cage exploded. The shockwave threw the three witches backward. Vijay rose slowly. The wounds on his body remained open for a moment. Then the bleeding slowed. Torn flesh began to close. The warriors watched in disbelief as the damage they had inflicted disappeared before their eyes.

“He cannot heal that quickly,” one vampire whispered.

“He should not be able to heal at all,” Mahakaali said.

Vijay heard her. He looked at the blood on his hands. For a fraction of a second, confusion crossed his face. Then he looked at the soldiers, and the confusion disappeared.

“Who is next?”

Nobody moved. Vijay smiled without warmth.

“Come on!”

He struck the floor with his foot. A wave of dark energy raced across the chamber, throwing the remaining warriors off balance. He moved through them with terrifying speed. Every warrior who attacked him was answered with overwhelming force. Those who raised weapons fell. Those who attempted spells found their magic turning against them. Those who fled discovered that the exits were no longer where they remembered them.

Vijay was not simply defeating the army. He was dismantling its confidence, its discipline, its belief that the three kingdoms could protect themselves. Blood stained the marble. Smoke filled the air. Death seemed to dance through the chamber, moving from banner to banner, leaving silence in its wake. The royal guard began to retreat.

Mahakaali screamed above the chaos.

“STOP ATTACKING HIM!”

Nobody listened. They were too frightened to obey. And every fresh attack pushed Vijay deeper into the rage that none of them understood.

Rudraveer could no longer watch. His eyes turned completely black. The shadows surrounding his body expanded until they covered the chamber walls.

“VIJAY!”

The young man turned. The Vampire King attacked. He moved faster than any ordinary creature could follow, striking Vijay across the chest with a blade of condensed darkness. Vijay staggered. Rudraveer struck again, then again. For several seconds, the Vampire King drove him backward.

A cheer rose from the surviving soldiers. Rudraveer summoned a massive shadow beneath Vijay and forced him to his knees.

“Look at me! You may have frightened my soldiers, but you will never rule my kingdom!”

Vijay's head hung forward. Blood dripped onto the floor. Rudraveer raised his weapon.

“Your father should have taught you obedience.”

Vijay stopped moving. The entire chamber seemed to grow colder. Slowly, he lifted his head.

“What did you say?”

Vijay's fingers closed around the shadow blade. Cracks spread through it. The Vampire King tried to pull away. He could not. Vijay stood. The shadow blade shattered in his hand. Then he struck Rudraveer with a force that sent the king crashing through the edge of the royal platform. Stone collapsed. The Vampire King hit the floor and did not rise immediately. His royal armour was broken. Blood ran from his forehead.

The soldiers stared at their fallen ruler. Vijay stepped toward him.

“Say his name again.”

Vijay seized him by the front of his armour.

“Tell me what you did to my father!”

A thunderous roar interrupted them. Bhairava transformed. His body expanded, his muscles hardened, and enormous claws tore through his armour. In moments, the Werewolf Lord stood in his ancient battle form, towering above the warriors around him.

He charged. Vijay released Rudraveer just before Bhairava struck him. The two collided. The impact shattered the remaining windows. Bhairava drove Vijay through a stone pillar and slammed him against the floor. For the first time, Vijay cried out in pain.

The Werewolf Lord attacked relentlessly. Claw after claw tore through Vijay's coat and skin. Bhairava's eyes burned with fury.

“You want answers? Then survive long enough to earn them!”

He threw Vijay across the chamber. The young man crashed against the wall and fell among the broken stones. Bhairava roared triumphantly. But the sound died in his throat.

Vijay was already standing. His chest rose and fell. Blood covered him. His left arm hung unnaturally at his side. Then, slowly, the arm straightened. The bone reset. The wound closed.

Vijay looked at Bhairava. Something terrible flickered behind his eyes.

“You should not have done that.”

Bhairava charged again. Vijay met him head-on. He caught the Werewolf Lord's first strike, twisted aside from the second, and drove a devastating blow into Bhairava's chest. The enormous warrior staggered. Vijay struck again. Bhairava fell to one knee. A final impact sent him crashing across the marble floor.

The Werewolf Lord tried to stand. His legs failed him. Vijay approached him.

“You told me to leave.”

He looked down at Bhairava.

“Now you can watch me decide whether I will.”

Bhairava raised his head, blood on his lips. For the first time, the Werewolf Lord looked at Vijay not as a child, not as a threat, but as something he could not defeat—something he might never understand.

Behind them, Rudraveer struggled to rise. The Vampire King and Werewolf Lord had attacked together. Both had fallen. And Vijay was still standing.

Mahakaali had seen ancient wars. She had watched armies disappear beneath storms of magic. She had imprisoned creatures that had survived the birth of mountains. But she had never witnessed anything like this.

Vijay's body was covered in wounds. His breathing was uneven. His clothes were soaked with blood. Yet every injury slowly healed, and every attempt to stop him seemed to make him more dangerous.

The Queen looked at the fallen rulers, then at her surviving warriors, then at Vijay. He was walking toward her. Slowly. Deliberately.

“Mahakaali.”

Her name sounded different in his mouth. Not like a title. Like an accusation.

“Tell me where my mother is.”

The Queen raised her staff.

“Vijay, listen to me.”

“WHERE IS SHE?”

His shout shook the chamber. Mahakaali struck the floor with her staff. A ring of white light erupted around them.

“Sleep beneath the roots of the first world. Blood beneath the moon of the last. Let the body remember what the soul has forgotten!”

A wave of magic struck Vijay. He collapsed. His hands hit the floor. His eyes rolled upward. For several seconds, he lay motionless.

Mahakaali gasped. The spell had worked. She had reached beyond his physical strength and attacked the connection between his body and his consciousness.

“Hold him!” Rudraveer shouted.

The Queen raised one trembling hand. White roots of magical light spread around Vijay's body, pinning him to the floor. His heartbeat slowed. His eyes closed. Mahakaali whispered the final words.

“Remain beneath the silence.”

Vijay stopped breathing. The chamber fell silent. One second passed. Two. Three.

Then Vijay's fingers moved. Mahakaali froze. A golden line appeared beneath his closed eyelids. The magical roots began to blacken.

“No,” she whispered.

Vijay lifted his head. The roots shattered. He inhaled sharply, rolled onto his side, coughing blood, and pushed himself upright. For a moment, he appeared genuinely exhausted. But his eyes were burning.

“You keep making the same mistake,” he said weakly. “You think if your magic works once, it will work forever.”

He stood unsteadily. Mahakaali stared at him.

“I have bound your body. I have attacked your mind. I have separated your consciousness from your blood. How are you still alive?”

Vijay looked down at his hands.

“I don't know.”

He raised his eyes.

“But I'm beginning to think you are afraid to find out.”

Then he attacked. Mahakaali barely raised her staff in time. Vijay's strike sent her sliding across the floor. She caught herself against a pillar, pain flashing across her face.

The Witch Queen had spent centuries mastering magic. Yet she was being forced backward by a young man whose power seemed to change every time she touched it.

She cast another spell. Vijay dropped to his knees. She cast a second. He collapsed onto one hand. She cast a third. His entire body froze. Every time, the magic worked. And every time, it began to fail. A minute. Sometimes less. Each spell bought her a few precious moments. Nothing more.

Mahakaali's hands began to shake. Vijay rose again. The Queen stared at him in horror.

“Why won't you stay down?”

Vijay's face was empty. His voice was almost a whisper.

“Because I remember the fire.”

He took one step forward.

“I remember the screams.”

Another step.

“I remember begging for someone to save me. And nobody came.”

His eyes filled with golden light.

“So now, when someone tells me to stop… I remember that nobody stopped them.”

Mahakaali lowered her staff for half a second. She understood something then. Vijay was not simply fighting them. He was reliving the night that had created him. Every weapon reminded him of the soldiers who surrounded his childhood home. Every spell reminded him of the witches who had covered the sky. Every command to surrender brought him back to the moment when three kingdoms had decided that a child deserved to die.

His rage was feeding something inside his blood. And that something was learning.

Mahakaali raised her staff again, but her hands were trembling too violently to complete the next incantation. Vijay advanced.

“Move.”

The Queen stood her ground.

“No.”

“Move, Mahakaali.”

“I will not allow you to destroy this world.”

Vijay stared at her.

“Then you should have protected it when you had the chance.”

He raised his hand. A dark flame appeared in his palm, red at its center and golden at its edges. The Queen felt every magical barrier in the fortress begin to collapse. If Vijay released that flame, she might not have enough power left to save anyone—not herself, not the kings, not even Vijay.

Mahakaali looked at the white oak staff in her hand. It had belonged to the first Witch Queen. According to legend, its roots had grown from the heart of the fourth kingdom, and its wood could command a power older than the three bloodlines.

But the staff alone was not enough. The spell required a sacrifice: the caster's own blood. Not an offering, not a drop placed upon an altar. The Queen's living blood had to become the anchor of the spell. If she failed, the magic would consume her. If she succeeded, the target would enter a sleep deeper than ordinary death—a state in which the body could neither heal nor awaken without the proper counterspell.

Mahakaali had never used it. The spell had been forbidden for centuries, because it could imprison a creature whose power was too great for any ordinary chain.

Vijay's dark flame grew larger. The chamber's remaining torches exploded. Mahakaali heard Bhairava trying to rise. She heard Rudraveer struggling beneath the broken stone. She heard the frightened breathing of the surviving warriors. And beneath every sound, she heard Vijay's heartbeat—fast, violent, unnatural.

The Queen made her decision. She drew the edge of her own staff across her palm. Blood appeared. The moment it touched the white oak, the staff began to glow. A circle of ancient symbols opened beneath her feet.

Vijay noticed the change.

“What are you doing?”

Mahakaali did not answer. She pressed her bleeding palm against the staff and whispered words that had not been spoken since the fourth kingdom disappeared. The air became heavy. Every shadow in the chamber turned toward her. The fallen warriors began crawling away from the circle.

Even Rudraveer looked frightened.

“Mahakaali! What spell is that?”

“The only one I have left.”

Vijay lunged. She raised the white oak staff. He struck her before she could finish the incantation. The impact sent her crashing against the altar. Her staff slipped from her hand. For one terrible second, the magic died.

Vijay stood over her, blood covering his face, his eyes blazing like two dying stars.

“Enough!”

He reached for her. Mahakaali grabbed the staff. With the last of her strength, she drove it forward. The white oak touched Vijay's chest. He recoiled. A thin line of white light spread from the point of contact.

Then Mahakaali pressed the staff against the center of his chest, directly over his heart. Her bleeding hand tightened around the wood.

“By the blood I surrender…”

The light spread.

“By the root that remembers…”

Vijay screamed—not in anger, but in pain. His body convulsed as if something inside him had suddenly awakened and was trying to escape.

“By the sleep that holds the first king…”

Vijay seized the staff. His fingers began crushing the wood. Mahakaali's face twisted with effort.

“LET THE BLOOD FALL SILENT!”

A white explosion engulfed the chamber. Every warrior was thrown to the floor. The royal banners burst into flames. The marble cracked from wall to wall. And Vijay stood at the center of the light, unable to move.

“What… did you… do?”

Mahakaali could barely speak.

“I am sorry.”

Vijay tried to lift his hand. His fingers trembled. His knees buckled. The dark flame vanished from his palm. His eyes lost their golden light. Then the red faded. For the first time since his return, Vijay looked like an ordinary young man—exhausted, wounded, human.

He stared at Mahakaali.

“Don't…”

His voice broke.

“Don't let them…”

He tried to finish the sentence. But his body could no longer obey him. Vijay collapsed. The white oak staff slipped from the Queen's hand. The chamber went dark.

Nobody moved. For several seconds, the only sound was the storm outside. Then Mahakaali crawled toward Vijay. She pressed two fingers against his neck. A heartbeat. Weak. Almost impossible to detect. But it was there.

She looked at his wounds. They were no longer healing. The bleeding had stopped. His body had become unnaturally still. The Queen placed her hand against his chest. The white oak's magic had drained the visible power from his blood. The dark flame was gone. The golden light had disappeared. Even the ancient presence that had surrounded him seemed to have vanished.

But something about the silence frightened her more than his rage had. Vijay's blood was not merely dormant. It was being held. Every drop seemed trapped inside a spell that was slowly pulling the life from his body.

Mahakaali looked at her bleeding palm, then at the broken staff. She had stopped him. But she had also begun something she did not know how to reverse.

“Is he dead?” Rudraveer asked.

“No,” Mahakaali replied.

“Then kill him,” Bhairava said.

The Queen's head snapped toward him.

“I said no!”

Her voice echoed through the chamber. Bhairava stared at her.

“He nearly destroyed all three kingdoms!”

“And if I had not stopped him, he would have destroyed himself as well!”

The words silenced the room. Mahakaali looked back at Vijay.

“He is not simply healing his wounds. Something in his blood is rebuilding him. Every time we injure him, that power learns. Every time we bind him, it adapts. If we kill him without understanding what he carries, we may release something far worse than the boy.”

Rudraveer wiped blood from his face.

“You expect us to let him live?”

“No. I expect us to keep him alive until we discover how to stop him permanently.”

Bhairava looked toward the dark passage.

“And if he wakes?”

Mahakaali glanced at the white oak staff. Her face grew pale.

“Then I may not be able to stop him again.”

Beneath the fortress lay a network of tunnels older than any of the three kingdoms. They had been built from black stone taken from the ruins of the fourth kingdom. Their walls absorbed light, and their passages twisted through the earth like the roots of a dead tree.

No ordinary creature could find the entrance. No ordinary magic could reveal the deepest chamber. And no prisoner had ever escaped from the place once its inner seal was closed.

The rulers carried Vijay down through the tunnels. His body lay upon a stone platform. His arms were bound with silver chains. Ancient runes covered the floor around him. The white oak staff had been placed beside the entrance, its light reduced to a faint glow.

Vijay did not move. His face was pale. His chest rose so slowly that the guards struggled to see it.

The Queen walked beside the platform, supporting herself against the wall. The spell had taken more from her than she had expected. Her own blood had become part of the binding. Every step sent pain through her arm, and the white oak's power had left her unable to sense the magical currents that had guided her for centuries.

“How long will he remain asleep?” Bhairava asked.

“I don't know,” Mahakaali replied.

“Days? Years?”

The Queen shook her head.

“I said I don't know!”

Bhairava fell silent. Rudraveer approached the platform. He looked down at the young man who had broken his army, shattered his pride, and nearly killed him.

“Twenty years ago, we should have made certain he died.”

Mahakaali turned toward him.

“And what would that have changed? We would still have a missing mother, a missing father, a prophecy we never understood, and a fourth kingdom waiting beneath our feet.”

Bhairava looked toward the dark passage.

“What do you suggest?”

“We search every forbidden archive. Every royal vault. Every surviving record of the fourth kingdom. And we discover why the white oak can stop his blood when no other spell can.”

“Why not destroy the body while he sleeps?” Rudraveer asked.

Mahakaali looked at him with cold fury.

“Because I do not know what will happen if we do.”

She stepped closer to the platform.

“And I will not gamble the existence of three kingdoms on your fear.”

For a long moment, nobody spoke. Then Bhairava gave the order.

“Seal the tunnel.”

The guards lowered the enormous stone door. One lock. Two locks. Three ancient seals. The final door closed with a sound that seemed to travel through the entire mountain.

Vijay was imprisoned. The three kingdoms had won. At least, that was what they wanted to believe.

Hours passed. Above the fortress, the storm finally ended. The surviving warriors returned to their posts. Healers treated the wounded kings. The royal mages repaired the shattered barriers. The official announcement was simple: Vijay had been captured. The kingdoms were safe.

But Queen Mahakaali refused to celebrate. She remained in the underground chamber, watching the prisoner through a narrow opening in the sealed door. Vijay lay motionless. The silver chains did not move. The runes remained dark. His blood had been drained from the surface of his wounds, leaving his skin pale and cold.

Mahakaali pressed her hand against the stone.

“Vijay.”

No response.

“I know you can hear me.”

Silence.

“I do not know what your father did. I do not know what happened to your mother. And I do not know why the fourth kingdom chose your blood. But I know what we did to you.”

For a moment, she could no longer look at him.

“I am sorry.”

She turned to leave. Then—a sound, so faint that she thought she had imagined it. A heartbeat. Mahakaali stopped. She turned back. Vijay's chest remained still. His face did not change. But beneath the skin over his heart, a tiny golden light appeared.

One pulse. Then darkness.

The Queen slowly approached the opening. Another pulse. This time, a thin red line spread beneath the golden light. The white oak staff, resting beside the door, began to tremble.

“No…”

The runes surrounding the chamber flickered. The chains tightened. The stone door shuddered. Deep inside the prison, Vijay's fingers moved once, then stopped. His eyes remained closed. But somewhere inside his sleeping mind, a voice began to speak—not his mother's, not his father's, not the voice of any living creature. An ancient voice. A voice that had waited beneath the earth for a thousand years.

“The first blood has returned.”

The golden light pulsed again.

“The first prison has opened.”

A crack appeared across the floor.

“Now wake the fourth king.”

Mahakaali ran from the tunnel. She did not stop until she reached the highest tower of the Moonspire fortress. There, she opened the oldest magical book in existence. Its pages were blank until she placed her bleeding palm upon them.

Words appeared in black ink.

THE WHITE OAK CAN SILENCE THE BLOOD.

A second line emerged.

IT CANNOT DESTROY WHAT THE BLOOD REMEMBERS.

Mahakaali turned the page. A third line appeared.

WHEN THE SLEEPING HEIR AWAKENS, THE FOURTH KINGDOM WILL RISE.

The Queen staggered backward. She remembered the old prophecy. Three kingdoms had tried to destroy one child. Three rulers had tried to control one bloodline. And now they had imprisoned the very person who might have been keeping something far worse asleep.

A final sentence appeared at the bottom of the page.

THE FIRST BETRAYAL WAS NOT COMMITTED BY A KING.

Mahakaali's breath caught. She had seen those words before. But beneath them, a new line began to form.

THE SECOND BETRAYAL WILL BE COMMITTED BY THE QUEEN.

The book slammed shut. Every candle in the tower went out. Far below, beneath the sealed fortress, Vijay's heart beat once more. The stone door cracked. The silver chains trembled. And somewhere beyond the borders of the three kingdoms, a golden mask turned toward Rudhiravan.

“At last.”

The masked man raised his hand. Behind him, an enormous army emerged from the darkness.

“Bring me my son.”

END OF EPISODE 3

The white oak has silenced the blood—but it cannot destroy what the blood remembers.
